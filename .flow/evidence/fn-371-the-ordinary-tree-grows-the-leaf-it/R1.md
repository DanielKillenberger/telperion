# fn-371 R1: the ordinary tree's leaf, as written and as drawn

Builder, 14 Nov 2026, on `fn-371-the-ordinary-tree-grows-the-leaf-it` at `e8dd2f45c` (the merged candidate, before any change). The numbers come from fn-370's leaf probe, copied to `leaf-probe/`. It now also prints the mean blade computed from the rows, and with `ROWS=1` the element rows. The raw lines are in the ignored `raw/before/` folder.

## The `leafArea` rows

`crates/telperion-core/presets/ordinary.values` holds 15 rows, `/space/ages/0/leafArea` to `/space/ages/14/leafArea`. All 15 read `0.00157` m², which is 15.7 cm².

- **Source:** commit `157ff25a9`, "feat(presets): the ordinary tree's leaf is 15.7 cm² (fn-354 task 7)", 12 Nov 2026. `git blame` gives that commit for every row and for its comment.
- **Comment:** "fn-354 task 7 (host, 12 Nov 2026): every age's leaf is 15.7 cm² (`leafArea`), the mean of the oak's 18.1, the beech's 15.7 and the birch's 13.2; estimated: the ordinary tree is a deciduous broadleaf, as its `leafYears` already says, and the corners' point (1.67 cm², between a needle, a blade and a frond) is no tree's leaf."
- **Earlier value:** the corners' centroid, 1.67 cm², written by `examples/centroid.rs`. That generator now writes no row: run again, it would replace every `/space` row with the centroid, including the hand-set `leafYears`, sapwood, sag and leaf rows. fn-354 task 7 therefore edited the rows by hand, and this change does the same.
- **Note:** the comment calls 15.7 the mean of three leaves, but the mean of 18.1, 15.7 and 13.2 is 15.67. The value equals the beech's measured 15.723 cm², rounded.

## The drawn blade, computed from the rows

The ordinary preset states no `/element` row and no `/canopy` row, so every value below is the catalogue's default.

- **Element** (`ElementParams`, as `ROWS=1` prints it): length 0.12 m, width 0.06 m, widestAt 0.42, baseFullness 0.85, tipSharpness 1.6, cup 0.18, curl 0.12, no lobes, connectorLength 0.01, 5 axial and 2 cross segments, no card.
- **Unit area:** 42.509 cm². This is the one-sided area of the element's blade triangles at scale 1, as the pipeline builds them (`build_element`, read through the expansion's `element()`). It is 0.590 of the 0.12 × 0.06 m bounding rectangle (72 cm²).
- **`/canopy/size`:** 1, the default.
- **`/canopy/sizeVariation`:** 0.35, the default.
- **Draw rule** (`foliage/leaflet.rs`, `drawn`): `scale = size × (1 + v × U(−1, 1))`, with U uniform. A blade's area goes as `scale²`, and the mean of `(1 + vU)²` is `1 + v²/3`.
- **Mean drawn blade:** 42.509 × 1² × (1 + 0.35²/3) = 42.509 × 1.040833 = **44.245 cm²**, which is 0.0044245 m².
- **Check:** the mean blade actually drawn (drawn area over instances) is 44.10 to 44.60 cm² on the eight trees measured. The rule gives 44.245.
- **Against the old value:** 44.245 / 15.7 = **2.818** (fn-370 measured 2.81 to 2.84).

## The ordinary tree's sapwood row

All 15 ages carry `/space/ages/N/form/sapwood = 3.021148036253776`, which is 1 / 0.331. This is 1 cm² of sapwood per 0.331 m² of leaf, the beech's value (Bartelink 1997), estimated for the ordinary tree. `sapwoodRings = 50.0` (estimated from Gebauer et al. 2008's range of 25 to 73). Both came from commit `a3e16468e` (fn-354 R6 step 1), and this change leaves both alone. Under the sapwood rule, the trunk's sapwood follows the leaf it carries. A leaf 2.8 times larger with the same row should give a trunk about √2.8 ≈ 1.68 times wider, less whatever the light takes back.
