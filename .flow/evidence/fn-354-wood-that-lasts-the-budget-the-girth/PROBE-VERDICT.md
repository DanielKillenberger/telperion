- **The trunk is a real oak's.** 27 to 34 cm at 40, 68 to 93 at 80, 106 to 139 at 120, 132 to 171 at 200, 145 to 185 at 300, against White's 28 to 40, 56 to 79, 83 to 109, 121 to 152, 156 to 192. Seven of fifteen trees are inside the range and the rest within about a quarter of it; today's are two to three times too thick to 120 and thin after. It never thins. The first tree came out at 27 cm against the paper's 30.
- **G2 draws every tree and every one stands.** G1 refuses one and lays limbs out wide and low; soft wood on leaf-sized limbs is wrong. B confirms what was known: the budget fix alone collapses the old oak (7.7 m at 300 on seed 1001) and is refused once.

## The oak under G2, tree by tree against the base

- **40: better.** A slim young tree in proportion; the heavy branches that littered the ground at the base are now twigs. The crown is a little thinner.
- **80: the same or better.** The same outlines on a trunk a third as thick. Seed 1007 loses its 2 m trunk with a collar and reads as a full domed tree.
- **120: better on seeds 1007 and 1012, neither better nor worse on 1001.** Upright single trunks under round crowns leafed through, where the base leans off its foot with rings on its trunk. What is lost: heft. The limbs are many, thin and straight, a fan from low on the trunk; leafless it reads as a broom, not as an oak's few heavy crooked limbs. On seed 1001 the base's thick-leadered V has more of an oak's character.
- **200: better, and not yet good.** Upright trees of 28 to 32 m on a 1.3 to 1.7 m trunk where the base is a bent bare pole. The crown is thin and made of wavy fine limbs, and single long bare shoots stand out of it.
- **300: not worse, and not acceptable.** It stands on a thick short trunk where the base loops on the ground. But the trunk is a lathe-turned post: a wide cylinder with a flat rim, then a much narrower stem banded with ring steps; seed 1007 carries a cut-off stub; the crown is small and the tree has shrunk to 18 to 24 m.

So against the bar "no tree worse than the base": met at every age by the host's eye, with 120 on seed 1001 the closest call. Against "the oak at 200 and 300 reads as an old oak": not met.

## What is wrong, by cause

1. **Steps where the section changes along a stem** (the flat rim under the fork at 120 in every variant, B included; the post and the banded rings at 300). The rule is right that the wood below a limb is thicker than above it. The drawing turns each change into a step over one internode. This needs a drawing rule and is in this spec. The flat-topped cylinder under a living fork is older than this spec (fn-279, "a fork is one surface").
2. **Limbs many, thin and straight.** Sections now add by area, as measured trees do, where the old exponent (2.6 to 2.8) kept limbs thicker than their share. So the count of limbs shows: twenty-odd of equal rank from low on the trunk, where an oak keeps a few. That is the tree's architecture (which limbs win, which are shed), not its girth, and it is Phase 3's with the crown. Straightness: stiff wood holds the angle a limb grew at; an oak's crooked limbs come from how it grows, not from sagging.
3. **The old crown is thin and the tree shrinks after 200.** Known; Phase 3.
4. **The drawn leaf moves with the girth** (the oak at 40 on seed 1001 draws 91 m² under G against 125 under B, while the grower's leaf is the same). The drawing places leaves by its own rule on wood under a radius bound. It is the leaf account's fault (below).
5. **The other species' trunks** (beech 9 to 12 cm, birch 17 to 19, spruce 39 to 42 at 40) follow each species' leaf as the grower counts it, which is not its standing leaf: the count leaves out the short-shoot clusters that carry most of a beech's and a birch's leaf (drawn leaf 2.6 to 3.8 times the rule's) and counts six years of needles on the spruce by a probe estimate. **Corrected the same day (`LEAF-ACCOUNT.md`):** the grower does count a short shoot's leaves, every year it lives; the beech's gap is blade size in the drawing and too few shoots in the grower, and what the grower really lacks is leaves older than a year (the spruce).
6. **Cost:** the yearly pass took 6, 11 and 14% of the grow at 40, 120 and 300, unoptimised, measured under load.

## Decisions

- **Stage 2 (twelve seeds of the probe) is not run.** It would cost about 2.5 hours to measure a rule the product will measure on the same twelve seeds at R8. The three seeds answered the question the probe was for.
- **R6 goes ahead** as `PRODUCT-DESIGN.md` says: the rule, the sag from wood's stiffness, the drawing's easing of a section change, the yearly pass folded into the grower.
- **R5 is narrowed to the grower's side:** one definition of the leaf a phytomer bears in a year (its standing leaf for as long as the species keeps a leaf; no cluster term, see the correction above), read by the light and by the wood. Making the drawn leaf the same leaf is Phase 5's, where the drawing of foliage is rebuilt from the grower's counts; building it twice would be waste. A design survey comes first.
- **R7 (the beech's branching) waits for R5's result,** since the missing clusters are part of the beech's missing leaf.
- **Not judged yet:** the beech, birch and spruce under G2. Their sheets are viewed once R5 changes their leaf, since their trunks today rest on a leaf count known to be wrong.
