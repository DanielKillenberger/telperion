# fn-308 R1: the trace, the curves and three candidates

Run on 2026-10-22 at `911262a6e` plus the probe `crates/telperion-core/tests/walk_midpoint.rs` (ignored tests). Every cargo run went through `telperion-heavy 8 --threads 2`. The drawn tree is `telperion_space::grow(growth(family))`, which is what `pipeline/space.rs` grows. "Share" is the lateral share of wood length (axes of order 1 or more). "Laterals" counts lateral axes that have wood. Logs and full tables are in `raw/` (`r1-*`, `c1-*`, `c2-*`, `c3-*`). The candidate patches are in `candidates/`. None is committed.

## Verdict

The age-2 lifespan inference holds at t = 0.5, but it is not the main cause. The drawn oak is already 93 % gone by t = 0.25 in wood and 98 % gone in lateral count. That happens long before age 2's lifespan passes the tree's 40 years at t ≈ 0.47.

The main cause is the branching rows at the ages the palm never reaches (3 to 14). `Species::canonical` writes those ages as placeholders: lateral and dormant probabilities 0, and readiness copied from the palm's age 2, which is 0. fn-299's rule swaps only the timing rows, so these placeholders still mix linearly. At every order of the oak, readiness × lateral probability shrinks by (1 − t)². The oak branches to order 9, so the crown shrinks geometrically in t. It is a steep front-loaded fade, not a step. The other five walks show no such fade.

- **At t = 0.5 the stem never leaves age 2.** That confirms the lifespan inference. The crown ages are never entered. The only laterals are age 2's own sprigs (age 8): 3 drawn, 1 living. The oak sheds these in any case: at t = 0 all 15 of the trunk's sprigs are shed by 40 ("the bole clears"). The side shoot the owner saw is that living sprig.
- **Each oak lateral is absent for one of two reasons.** Laterals of ages 3, 4, 6 and 7 are absent because their bearer ages are never entered (the lifespan). Even where those ages are entered, as at t = 0.25, every order's lateral rate is (1 − t)² of the oak's, because of the placeholder branching rows.
- **A third lever is age 2's continuation.** It is an `Outcome` row and mixes as 1 − t. Per seed, the stem's draw then decides whether the stem moves on at all. Seed 42 under C1 stops at t = 0.5 and stays a bare stem.
- **The limbs inherit the palm's girth.** The palm's placeholder `form.pipe` at ages 3 to 14 is its stem's 0.297 m, against the oak limb's 0.009 m. On the log scale the midpoint is about 0.05 m. The base never draws these ages at t = 0.5. Candidate 3 does, and its limbs are fat (see its still).

### C1: blend the rate, `lifespan` on the `Kept` scale, 1/(1 + L) mixed by value (one table row)

This is the rate with the same one-year floor as the old Log scale. A literal 1/L would hold a pass-through age, where L is 0 at one end, at 0 until t = 1 and then switch.
- **Effect on oak to palm.** The stem enters the crown ages until t ≈ 0.75, where L2 reaches 40. The drawn tree barely moves, because the branching placeholders still damp every order. At t = 0.5, seed 1 gives 16 laterals and 32 m. Seed 42 stops its stem on the continuation draw and draws a bare stem (share 0.022).
- **Still `raw/stills/c1-t0.5.png`.** It shows the base palm with one leader limb beside the crown.
- **What else it moves.** `catalogue_identity` re-pins. In `space_walks::the_grown_count_binds_where_a_blend_sheds_more_than_its_ends`, the test fails and prints 0.623 and 0.882 (base green). Every walk's lifespans move: on beech to spruce at 0.5, L2 goes from 18.0 to 14.7, and the wood moves 5,036 → 3,882 m at seed 1. `units_bound` (fn-299's units), `walk_law`, `walk_leaves`, `walk_sizes`, `blend_reach` and `walks` stay green.

### C2: weight the lifespan blend by each end's share of the tree's age in the stage

The rule is t′ = t·s_b / ((1 − t)·s_a + t·s_b), with s = min(L, age) / age. This needs the age passed into `telperion_space::blend`.
- **Effect on oak to palm.** It moves the wrong way. The palm spends the whole 40 years in age 2 and the oak only 12, so the palm's lifespan dominates: L2 is 84.7 at t = 0.5 and 38.6 already at t = 0.2. The t = 0.5 frame is identical to the base. The still `raw/stills/c2-t0.5.png` is byte-identical to `base-t0.5.png`. Fewer laterals are drawn earlier, for example 450 against 722 at t = 0.25.
- **A switch at the passing end.** At an age where one end has L = 0, s = 0, so t′ = 1 for any t > 0. `space_walks::the_size_is_neutral_at_either_end_and_continuous_off_it` fails: the beech's size at t = 10⁻⁶ is 49,387 against 180,095.
- **What else it moves.** `units_bound::the_floor_counts_the_ages_a_cycle_chains` goes from 4 to 2, so fn-299's pass-through sliver moves. `catalogue_identity` re-pins. `the_grown_count_binds` fails and prints 1.000 and 0.775. Two `space_walks` tests fail only because they compare against the unaged `telperion_space::blend`.

### C3, from the trace: fn-299's swap also covers the branching rows (zones, readiness, rhythm), plus C1

The swap covers an age one end never reaches. The branching swap alone is `candidates/c3a-branching-only.patch`. Its 21-frame curve is in `raw/c3a-oak-palm-21.md`. At t = 0.5 it gives 856 laterals and 130 m, but the stem still never leaves age 2 there, and the share falls to 0 by t ≈ 0.65. So both levers are needed.
- **Effect on oak to palm.** At t = 0.5 there are 1,899 laterals and 255 m of wood, which is the ends' log-midpoint of about 206 m. With more laterals, fn-230's size factor now acts (0.617 on the lateral rows at t = 0.5). It halves the tree towards the ends' geometric mean, as fn-230 designed.
- **The fade moves later but does not go.** The share falls from 0.84 at t = 0.6 to 0 at t = 0.75 on seed 1, because L2 passes 40 at t ≈ 0.75. That is a steeper fall than the base has anywhere, so R3's step test would still fail near t = 0.7. Seed 7 fades earlier, from t ≈ 0.6, which is the age-2 continuation draw (inferred, not traced).
- **Still `raw/stills/c3-t0.5.png` (viewed).** A short, thick stem stands with a leafy crown of drooping leaflets. The fork's limbs are thick stubs, like fingers, because the palm's placeholder `form.pipe` (0.297 m) still mixes into the oak's limb ages. **It looks off:** it reads as a stubby bush on a palm trunk, not as a tree halfway between the two.
- **What else it moves.** `catalogue_identity` re-pins. `space_walks::a_walk_to_the_palm_is_never_grown_to_its_ends_size` fails, because host decision 1 says the walk to the palm stays unscaled, and under C3 the beech-to-palm midpoint is scaled. Beech to spruce and spruce to birch move as C1 moves them, through the lifespan. `units_bound`, `walk_law`, `walk_leaves`, `walk_sizes`, `blend_reach` and `walks` stay green.

### What the trace suggests but was not measured

These are for the host's ruling.
- **Every placeholder row, swapped.** A placeholder age carries a whole row set copied from the palm's age 2. That set includes `form.pipe`, which made C3's fat limbs. Swapping every row of an unreached age is fn-299's original rule B. The host narrowed that rule to the timing rows because of the leaf law (`cluster_leaves`).
- **A lifespan past the tree's age as dormant.** The settings table already says it is ("Dormant while the tree is younger than it"). Mixing min(L, age) would keep L2 under 40 until t = 1, and so remove C3's late fall. It needs the age in the blend, as C2 does.
- **The age-2 continuation.** It decides per seed whether the stem goes on at all. It remains a per-seed threshold under any lifespan rule.
