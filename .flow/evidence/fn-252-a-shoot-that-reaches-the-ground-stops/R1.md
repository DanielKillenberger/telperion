# fn-252 R1: the birch's crown base and the wood on the ground (worker, 2026-10-14)

Measured on `fn-252-birch-ground` at 60b1201d2, with `ground_measure.rs` (this folder; copied into `crates/telperion-core/examples/` to run). The probe grows each preset's engine species through `telperion_space::grow` with the preset's own light, then reads the structure. With `--leaves`, it also runs the pipeline's leaves. Raw logs are in `raw/`: `birch.log`, `birch80.log`, `birch-first.log`, `birch-leaves.log`, `others.log`, `others2.log` and `spruce80.log`. Stills are in `raw/stills/`. No rule or value was changed.

Definitions:
- **Crown base** is the base height of the lowest first-order branch whose subtree still holds a living apex. Short shoots on the stem are left out.
- **Resting** means a non-stem node at z < 1 mm. The lay clamps a resting step to exactly 0.
- PAs of the birch: 6 `LIMB`, 8 `SPRIG` (the young stem's passing branch), 9 `BRANCH` (the hanging strand), 14 `SHORT`.

- **The crown base does not lift between 5 and 30.** In every seed, the young stem's lowest passing branches, born in year 1, are still alive at 0.6-0.8 m when the tree is 30 and 13 m tall. At 40 one of the three seeds still has one at 0.72 m, now 39 years old.
- **The lowest branches are among the best-lit wood in the tree.** From age 10 on, their apices read as much light as the tree's median or more. They stand out from under the crown, so shade does not shed them.

### Why they persist (checked in code)

1. `SPRIG` has lifespan 14, viability 0.97 a year, and is shed 2 years after the last living apex in its subtree (`birch.rs`; `shed.rs`).
2. The strands a sprig bears (`BRANCH`) carry their own age on at 0.005 a node. `shed.rs` (`lasting`) states that a subtree's life is then unbounded: "Life is unbounded where a lateral bears its own age". So a sprig lives on as long as one strand in its subtree keeps forking. Measured: 3 to 40 living apices on the 29- to 39-year-old sprigs.
3. Shade does not shorten their life. The shade hazard is mapped from T 2.03, which gives 1.14 (`tolerance.rs`). It acts as `viability^(light^-hazard)` (`grow/advance.rs:72-75`). At their light of 0.85-0.98, the 0.97 viability becomes about 0.966-0.969 a year, almost unchanged.

## Why the lower strands carry few leaves (birch, seed 1 and 42; checked against code)

| 0-0.25 | 28 / 25, 1, 11 / 12 | 24 / 23, 4-5, 6 / 6 | 15 / 16, 7-8, 3.5 / 3.5 |
| 0.5-1 | 28 / 28, 2, 9 / 11 | 17 / 15, 6, 4 / 4 | 12 / 12, 8-14, 1.9 / 1.1 |

| 4-8 | 26 / 27, 1, 13 / 13 | 28 / 29, 3, 8 / 8 | 17 / 25, 6-8, 3.2 / 4.6 |
| 8-16 (crown) | - | 46 / 40, 0, 18 / 16 | 37 / 40, 3, 8.5 / 10 |

- **The leaves follow the living short shoots.** Each short shoot carries a cluster of 3 leaves, and leaves per metre run at about three times the living short shoots per metre. At 10, the strands are clothed evenly at every height. By 40, the strands below 8 m carry a third to a half of the crown's leaves per metre.
- **The low strands are old wood.** Their median wood age is 8-14 years at 40, against 3 in the crown.
- **Short shoots are made once and die out.** A strand's short shoots are its node buds (0.845 a medial node), set when the node grows. `SHORT` lives 6 years at viability 0.92 a year, has no laterals, and is not replaced. Old strand wood therefore loses its leaf clusters, at rows sourced in MODEL-BIRCH rule 8 (JH87: short shoots "at best only replace themselves").
- **Leaf retention plays no part.** The birch's `leaf_years` is infinite on every age, so `tree/leafing.rs` keeps every station.
- **Light plays no part.** The low wood reads high light (above), and the size response is dormant (fn-264).
- **The along-shoot stations add little low down.**
  - A node with children bears stations only within the slender bound, stem radius × `/canopy/shootRadius` 0.03 (`foliage/plan.rs`, `bearing_of`). A tip node bears them below 5% of the root's radius (`pipeline/space.rs:565`).
  - Measured against 3% of the root radius as a stand-in for the stem radius: 55-91% of the strand wood below 4 m is thicker than the bound at every age, including 10, where the leaves per metre are still even.
  - So the bound is not what changes with age. The short-shoot count is.

## Reading: the two causes told apart

1. **Lower branches the birch should already have shed. This is the main cause at 5 to 30.**
   - Every resting strand at 10 to 30 hangs from a `SPRIG` based 0.6-4.4 m up the stem. The crown base stays at 0.6-0.7 m until 30 in every seed.
   - The sprigs are meant to pass (MODEL-BIRCH rule 7, "the bole clears"). They persist for two reasons: their strands' self-bearing fork makes their subtrees' life unbounded (`shed.rs`), and the light-mapped shade hazard barely touches them because they stand in good light.
   - Remove those sprigs and the resting wood at 10-30 goes with them, with the stems clear to the next branches at 1.5-4 m.
   - The rule or value that lets a passing branch outlive its 14 years is the host's to choose:
     - the strand's 0.005 self-fork;
     - the sprig's viability or shedding;
     - a crown-lift rule not keyed to shade.
2. **Strands long enough to reach the ground from where they should be. This is the secondary cause at 40 and 80.**
   - At 40, the resting strands hang from branches based at 2.3-6.1 m, and at 80 from `LIMB`s at 5-10 m. A strand of 12 years at 4-6 nodes of 0.06 m reaches 3-4 m, more with forks, so wood hung that high still reaches the ground.
   - The amount is smaller, 2-23 m of wood at 40 and 0-8 m at 80, but the 40-year stills show the root-like runs the owner saw: thick, old strands laid along the soil and curving there.
   - This part is the ground rule (R2) and its open design point.

The young birch's "drooping all the way to the ground" (owner, 2026-10-14) is therefore mostly cause 1. The 40-year roots in the owner's screenshot are cause 2 on top of the few surviving sprigs.
