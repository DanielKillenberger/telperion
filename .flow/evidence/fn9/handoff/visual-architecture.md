# Conductor visual observations

I inspected O-BARE (`quga999A.jpg`), S-BRANCH (`piab428B.jpg`) and the baseline bare render copied into the conductor `.refs/fn9/`.

Oak: stout trunk splits into several visibly substantial, crooked axes, with repeated changes in direction and subsequent subdivision. The crown is supported by a hierarchy of limbs, not long unbranched arcs carrying a halo of hairlike ends. Current baseline has exactly the latter issue: smooth tall arcs, sparse subdivisions and a narrow upper crown. Removing supernatural writhe is necessary but not sufficient; preserve natural, locally varied directions and meaningful subdivision.

Spruce: nearly horizontal primary with an upturned end supports many slender secondary axes hanging below it. The hanging structure is woody branching, not just a downward foliage orientation. Layered primary lengths form the cone; shortening an oak envelope alone cannot create this habit.

Both references require crown gaps at coherent branch/clump scales. Avoid uniformly filling or uniformly thinning a shell. The final QA must inspect attached twig close-ups; baseline isolated leaf capture cannot prove attachment fidelity.
