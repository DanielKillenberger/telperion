## Task .2: what bounds one cycle's deepening of the layout (2026-10-20)

Read in code at `488580cbd`, before any edit. Nothing was run. **The run stops `NEEDS_HUMAN`**: the code holds a bound tighter than `DEEP`, but it is not the bound the ruling's formula assumes, and the formula to take from it is a design call.

**The layout's depth is the depth of one cycle, not a depth that grows from cycle to cycle.** (Measured in code.)
- A new axis's layout generation counts only its ancestors that are new this cycle (`cycle/layout.wgsl:279-292`: `while a != NONE && a >= cfg.first_new`). `first_new` is `LAID0` (`plan_lay.rs:52`), which is the axes counted at the last cycle's end (`plan_lay.rs:281`, carried over at `build_plan.rs:181`).
- The marks `LAY_DEEP` reads are cleared at every cycle's start (`plan_lay.rs:90-94`).
- So `deepest` in `limits()` (`build_size.rs:210`) is the depth of the cycle two cycles back. It is not a base the next cycles add to. `laygens = deepest + slack over two cycles` models a depth that accumulates, and this one does not. The cycle needs the most depth one cycle can reach, whatever `deepest` was.

**What one cycle's depth is made of.** (Inferred from code, not measured.)
- The living apexes' jobs run first (`plan_units.rs:223`), then the sleeping buds' (`plan_units.rs:241, 244`). A living job chains its units, and each one after the first is a new axis one generation deeper (`build_arena.rs:123-129`).
- A sleeping bud wakes on every axis that carries its bearer on (`telperion-space/src/grow/wake.rs:40`, `dormant.rs:123-130`), so it wakes on the end of the chain the living job just made. Its own chain stacks on that one.
- One cycle's depth is therefore the living chain plus the wake chain, plus one for each failed unit's relay. `floors(units)` (`build_arena.rs:127`) states one job's chain, `units` generations. It does not state the two jobs stacked, and `most_units` leaves out the failure relays (`telperion-space/src/schedule.rs:260-269`: "a floor ..., never a bound"). That is why R1 saw depth 4 or more at cycle 9 while `floors` gave under 3.

**The bounds the code does enforce on one cycle**, tightest first:
1. The cycle's own `unit_rounds` limits (`build_size.rs:240-242`). A job with work left past the last recorded round is flagged (`units.wgsl:328`) and the build is refused by name, `UnitRounds` (`plan_units.rs:300`). If each round deepens a job's chain by one axis at most, which is inferred from the `floors` doc and not checked, one cycle's depth is at most `unit_rounds[0] + unit_rounds[1]` plus a constant. At t = 0.2160 those were [5, 5].
2. `FRAMES` = 11, the axes one units job holds (`device.rs:58`). That gives two jobs 22, which is no tighter than `DEEP`.
3. `DEEP` = 16 (`lay_device.rs:24`) and the plan's `LAY_GENERATIONS` = 15 (`build_arena.rs:112`).

**Why this goes back to the host.**
- The ruling's formula needs a per-cycle slack `k`. Taking `k` from `floors` gives `deepest + 2 * units`. That covers t = 0.2160 (1 + 4 >= 4) only because of the factor two, not because of any bound, so it is a guess.
- Taking `k` from the `unit_rounds` limits is a bound, if the one-axis-per-round reading holds. But `deepest + 2k` with k near 10 is the cap of 15 on every preset. That is the +15.0% `laygens`-at-bound cost in batch 2, past the acceptance's "within noise".
- A bound that does not accumulate, such as `laygens` from the cycle's own `unit_rounds` alone (for example `unit_rounds[0] + unit_rounds[1]`, capped at 15), does not fit the ruling's `deepest + slack` shape. Its cost was not measured either.
- The choice between these is a design call. No code was changed, and there is no fallback to `DEEP`.

## Task .2, step 1: what an empty layout generation costs (2026-10-20, revised ruling)

Measured before any change, at `e8470899a`. **Confirmed: an empty generation pass is dispatched over every new axis of the cycle and filters by generation, so it costs a fixed floor plus a share that grows with the cycle's new axes (42 to 83% of a full pass).** **The run stops `NEEDS_HUMAN`**: `cycle/` has no indirect dispatch, a host rule forbids one, and the trace shows the fixed floor alone would keep the small presets past noise even with one.

**From the code.**
- Every generation pass `g` in `0..laygens` records the same seven dispatch steps, each sized `over_new = groups(new / 64)`, where `new` is all of the cycle's new axes (`plan_lay.rs:140`, `145-163`). The steps are `flag`, the scan of `counts` over `new`, `gather`, `frame`, `frame_on` and `draw`.
- `flag_main` writes a word for every new axis, and `gather_main` reads every new axis's generation and exits unless it equals `cfg.generation` (`layout.wgsl:297-303`, `309-313`). `frame`, `frame_on` and `draw` exit past the generation's list. Nothing shrinks a pass whose generation holds no axis.
- Every dispatch in the cycle is direct (`plan.rs:4-8`). The only sizes are `Size::Direct` and `Size::Bounded` (`plan.rs:43-47`), and the latter is the host's largest value over its census span (`given.rs:199-205`). No `dispatch_workgroups_indirect` exists under `generation/` (grep). DESIGN.md (fn-256) §7a rule 1 (host, 2026-10-11): "Direct dispatches only. No `dispatchWorkgroupsIndirect` anywhere in the cycle, native included, so both paths share one shape". That rule rests on Chrome's 429 µs per indirect dispatch against 5 to 7 µs direct.

**The trace.** A scratch test (`raw/fn298_trace_tests.rs.scratch`, with `r2-scratch.patch`; diff in `raw/t2-trace-scratch.patch`, log `raw/t2-trace.log`, never committed) grew each preset at default age and seed 42 at its settled sizes (no moves). It ran with `laygens` stepped and then with `laygens` at its bound (15). Every dispatch was timestamped. Each generation pass's time was grouped by its index `g` and called full or empty from the cycle's own census (`LAY_DEEP[g]`). Wall times are three interleaved unstamped pairs.

- Each pass is 7 dispatches; the extra layout dispatches at the bound divide to 7.0 per extra pass on every preset.
- The cheapest empty pass anywhere is 32 µs, on the palm, whose cycles make the fewest new axes. That is the floor of 7 dispatches, about 4.6 µs each, stamped. Above it, an empty pass grows with the cycle's new axes: 41 µs on the beech and 245 µs on the spruce.
- Most of the wall delta is the device. The spruce's waiting rose 179 → 266 ms while its recording rose 54 → 57 ms. The palm's recording rose 28 → 37 ms, so host recording is a visible part of the cost on the smallest tree.

**What an indirect dispatch would and would not buy** (inferred from the table, not measured):
- A generation with no axes dispatched at 0 workgroups would remove the per-axis share: about 210 µs a pass on the spruce, and 10 to 20 µs on the beech and birch.
- It would not remove the floor. Each of the seven steps is still a dispatch with its barrier, and the scan is several. At about 32 µs stamped a pass, the beech's 441 extra passes cost about 14 ms, and its whole measured delta is 12.9 ms. The beech, birch and palm would stay past their noise band (R2 batch 2: the beech's spread is 3 ms). Stamped dispatches overstate the unstamped floor, by an amount not measured.
- In the browser, every indirect dispatch costs about 429 µs (§7a). Fifteen passes of seven dispatches is about 45 ms a cycle, against a cycle budget of 340 dispatches.

**Why this goes back to the host.**
1. The ruling's confirmed branch asks for an indirect dispatch. None exists in `cycle/`, and adding one reverses §7a rule 1. That is a design call the spec does not settle (binding instruction 2).
2. The trace says even a free 0-workgroup dispatch leaves the per-pass floor. So "`laygens` records every pass to its bound" would cost the small presets past noise whatever the dispatch kind, unless the empty passes are not recorded at all.
3. The ruling's other branch, `laygens = min(15, unit_rounds[0] + unit_rounds[1] + 1)`, was not built. Its cost depends on each preset's `unit_rounds` limits per cycle, which were not read here. With t = 0.2160's [5, 5] it is 11 of 15 passes, so it may cost close to the bound. Its premise (a units round adds at most one generation to a chain, relays included) is unchecked.

No production code was changed. The tree is clean.

**`rounds` (the hierarchy's generations, `plan_hier.rs:98-168`, `hier_blocks.wgsl`): not chain-local.** A generation's round places each waiting axis into a block, and where it lands depends on every other waiter of the same generation and on the blocks' shared fill. Only the depth (`depth_main`, `hier_blocks.wgsl:21-54`) is chain-local. Block membership, block numbers and spills are set by scans and ranks over the whole generation, and the next generation's targets read them (row 1), so one invocation per chain cannot lay the same blocks: a closing pass like (d) cannot express it bit-identically. Expressing the remaining generations in one dispatch would need a barrier across the generation between rounds, which a dispatch does not have. Cost guess, from R2 batch 2 (measured): `rounds` at its bound costs +2.8% in total (beech +9.7%, birch +7.0%, palm +14.5%). An unused round's dispatches are sized to 0 workgroups by `active` (`plan_hier.rs:111-112`), so its cost is the empty dispatches' floor, about 7 dispatches and two scans a pass.

**`unit_rounds[0]` (the living jobs' rounds, `plan_units.rs:196-220`, `units.wgsl`): job-local, so a closing pass may be expressible, but it is not a copy of (d).** Each round dispatches `DECIDE` over jobs, `ZONES` (and, emitting, `WRITTEN`, `SPROUTED`, `PLACED`) over the job's zones, and `AFTER` over jobs. Every read outside one job, with its line:

| # | Where | What | Enters a value? |

| 1 | `units.wgsl:30-49` (`bases`), `plan_units.rs:224-228` (the scans of `T_UNITS`, `T_UNITS_B` between the counted and the emitted pass) | each job's appends placed past the scanned counts of every job before it | yes, the indices written; but they come from the counted pass, whose counts a closing pass would have to complete before the scan |
| 2 | `units.wgsl:132` | `atomicMax` on `T_USED` | no: a tally, written only |
| 3 | `units.wgsl:328` (`close_main`) | `T_ROUNDS` flag | no: written only |
| 4 | `units.wgsl:156-174` (`zone_of`), `177-240` | a unit's zones run as separate invocations of the same job, and the emitted phases write "from the places the zones before it left" (`units.wgsl:186-188`) | within one job only |
