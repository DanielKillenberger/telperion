# Rethinking the generator, 10 Nov 2026

Asked by the owner on 9 Nov 2026: "Can you really take a step back and use your intellect to inspect the generator and rethink every part of it and find an approach that fits my goals and engineering criteria. Don't get bogged down by previous attempts. Don't just make another iterative step. Really inspect deeply with subagents (opus) aggregate info and internalize how everything works and then find solution approach that can be embedded in the goal criteria."

Seven Opus surveyors read the generator on 9 to 10 Nov, read-only, each one part: the growth cycle; the species model and the look; the path from the grown tree to pixels; the two executors and the code beside them; the history and 0.1.4; the published state of the art; the numbers. Their reports came back to the host's session and are condensed here with their own source marks. Everything under "The approach" is the host's proposal. None of it is owner-approved scope and none of it has been run.

## What the survey found

### The cost is twigs that are never seen

- Cost is flat at 1.1 to 1.7 µs per phytomer grown, with no hot pass, and past 80 it follows what is grown, not what is alive (fn-323, fn-330, the profile of 9 Nov).
- Of the phytomers grown, 65% are shed by 40, 80 to 82% by 80, 97.5% by 200 and 98.6% by 300 (fn-323, fn-330). Laterals that live under five years carry 73% of the oak's grown phytomers (fn-256 ROOFLINE).
- At 80, branching orders 4 and deeper are 93% of the apex-cycles; orders 0 to 2 are 1% (fn-323).
- In a 720-pixel still of a 40-year oak one pixel is about 2 cm. Every fine internode is under a pixel; only orders up to about 3 resolve as lines. There are about 3 kept phytomers and 100 wood triangles per crown pixel (computed from fn-336).
- The website's voxel style draws about 3,500 cubes for an oak from about a million phytomers grown, and its cost does not change with grid size (fn-198 FIELD-PATH, FIRST-PRINCIPLES-2026-10-31).
- If every year after 40 cost what year 40 costs, the oak would reach 300 in about 23 s (computed).

### The look is prescribed, not grown

- A tree's shape is written per physiological age as timers and angles: the oak's bole is a 12-year trunk stage, so the fork sits near 4.5 m whatever the light; its scaffold count is a fixed probability over two years; limb angles are fixed targets (`oak.rs`, `beech.rs`).
- Light only prunes. The one rule where light moves growth (`shadeSize`) is zero in five of six real species, and where it is on it is normalised inside each physiological age, so light never moves growth from the leader to the limbs (`allocation.rs:132-137`).
- Nothing limits height: the oak's limb stage lives 500 years at a constant internode. Nothing holds a limb's angle: sag is a beam bent once at the settle, about 80 times an elastic beam's, and growth never reads it.
- The architectural values are marked "tuned on the stills" (`SOURCES.md`), and some shipped values are fitted to hit a target: the beech's short-shoot lifespan to a leaf area index, the birch's pipe to a trunk diameter, the oak's `retained` to a trunk diameter.
- Against real trees: the spruce grows about 50 to 70 times the new shoots a year of a measured Norway spruce and is the costliest preset; the beech's leaf area index is 0.8 against 5 or more; the oak's trunk at 40 is about three times an oak of its height (estimates from fn-334's reference, fn-336 and published counts).
- Only the current year's leaves cast shade (`draw.rs:214-229`).

### The code carries a second grower that cannot do the job

- The device grower is about 25,000 lines (11.7k Rust, 13.6k WGSL) against the CPU grower's 6,000, with about 13,000 more that only hold the two in agreement. It is 4 to 7 times faster warm and is refused past age 95 on a 2 GiB buffer.
- The CPU grower pays for the agreement: f32 arithmetic with hand-written logs, a light lattice quantised "as device atomics would", sums in blocks of 1,024, 1,500 lines of captures.
- The owner's stills are drawn by the CPU grower and the harness by the device grower.
- The website's field entry is still device-only; the owner's choice of 1 Nov (CPU for every visitor) was never built.

### The tree is laid twice and a year is not a state

- Geometry is laid roughly each year for the light (15 to 17%) and never moved; the settle lays every phytomer again with the final bend and sag (13 to 15%). So the light reads a crown that is not the crown drawn.
- A tree at an age exists only after a settle, which copies the whole grower. Showing growth settles every shown age.
- A tree at year y depends on the age requested, through the presence windows (`presence.rs:48-66`): a 40-year tree inside an 82-year request is not the 40-year request's tree.

### What the previous engine and the literature say

- 0.1.4 was fast because it never simulated time. Height was an input, it ran no light and no carbon balance, and it capped a tree at 250,000 nodes. Its real species did not use space colonization (`attractorWeight` 0). Part of its speed is what the owner has since ruled out.
- Published growers get their speed in three ways: computing a substructure once and reusing it (GreenLab: 0.7 s against 4,743 s for a 15-year tree, lost once growth reads light per organ); computing light, vigour and shedding per branch module, not per bud (Makowski et al. 2019: trees of 99 complex or 1,204 simple modules judged visually alike; hundreds of thousands of plants); regenerating geometry on demand instead of storing it (Kuth et al. 2025).
- The Grove, the closest commercial grower, stops simulating at the last year's flush and instances a twig there.
- Posture is a present-state rule: gravitropic performance goes as diameter over height squared, a stem holds its lean only while it is 1 or more, and this limit binds before buckling (Alméras and Fournier 2009). A lengthening water path cuts assimilation and stops height (Ryan and Yoder 1997), with no published functional form.
- What reads as real, from a 4,000-person study: fork angles and the length and volume ratios between parent and child, ahead of crown outline; very thick and very thin branches read as unreal (Polasek et al. 2021).

### The pattern in everything tried

Every route kept the per-twig cycle as the truth and tried to copy it more cheaply, then was held to that cycle's own statistics. The cycle is not a truth worth matching: its form is prescribed by eye and several of its counts are far from real trees. What was never tried is changing the reference.

## The approach

**One law, evaluated three ways. Structure by buds, foliage by cohorts, twigs on demand.**

A species stays what it is: a point in the continuous space, its growth law the bud automaton over physiological ages. What changes is where that law is run bud by bud.

1. **The boundary is the light cell, not a tuned value.** The model's own light lattice (0.5 m, a site parameter) is the scale at which parts of a tree can tell each other apart: everything inside a cell deposits into, and reads from, the same number. A subtree that cannot grow out of one cell therefore interacts with the rest of the tree only through totals. That is the reach rule fn-334 found to hold with no row and no `first`, now with its reason. It moves by degree with the cell and with the species' own shoot lengths.

2. **Wood that outgrows a cell is grown bud by bud, and its fate is earned.** This is the trunk, limbs, boughs and branches: the few hundred to few thousand axes a view can resolve. Here the model gains what it lacks: vigour that moves between orders with light and sets how much a shoot grows; a lateral that becomes a limb because it is vigorous, not because a timer ran; a lean that holds while the wood's gravitropic performance allows; height that slows as the path from the root lengthens. Each is a published rule read from a branch's present state, with measured traits for values. Fewer values are set by eye, and none is fitted to a target.

3. **Foliage inside a cell is a cohort.** For each bearing stretch of wood: how many leafy shoots of each age it carries, their leaf, wood and section, moved each year by the same law in expectation (the expectation `closed_form` already computes from the same tables, over a shoot's own life), with the same shade and balance hazards read at the cell. Cohort counts are whole numbers drawn by lineage key, so trees keep their variation and stay repeatable. This is how botany itself counts organs (GreenLab's populations, the pipe model, leaf area index), and it is not a copy of the bud-by-bud cycle to be matched: it is the model.

4. **A twig is realised when something looks at it.** Any leafy shoot is a pure function of its lineage key, its birth year and its cell's light, grown by the same unit code. A close view realises the twigs it can see; a whole-tree view draws a cohort's leaf as density; the website's voxel field is the model's own cell totals. A realised twig is the same twig every year until its keyed death.

5. **One grower, one executor.** Growth runs on the CPU, native and Wasm, for every consumer. The device grower and everything that holds the CPU to it are deleted; the GPU draws. With foliage as cohorts the work per year follows the structure, so the reason for a device grower goes, and it could never reach 300.

6. **One geometry, and a year is a state.** Posture is a present-state rule, so wood is laid once and updated where it is still growing. The tree at any age is the grower's state, with no settle of a copy and no dependence on the requested age. Growth in view costs a draw, not a rebuild.

7. **Held to botany and the owner's eye.** Each species is checked against published counts (leaf area index, new shoots a year, trunk diameter against height, height against age with its plateau, crown width against height when open-grown) and the fork statistics that read as real, then by the owner on sheets. Today's cycle is kept through the change as an instrument to compare with, never as a gate, and is deleted when the new grower stands.

### What it should give, as estimates to be measured

- Work per year follows the axes that outgrow a cell. On the oak at 40, orders 0 to 3 hold about 11% of the live apexes; at 80, about 5% (fn-323). So an order of magnitude at 40 and more at old ages, before any engine work: an oak at 40 near 0.1 s on one thread, an oak at 300 in seconds to tens of seconds, a thousand oaks in about ten seconds on this desk.
- The growth code shrinks: about 38,000 lines of device growth and agreement go, and the CPU cycle loses its second arithmetic, its captures and its second lay.
- The website's field needs no drawn stage at all, which is the only route found to a 250 KB download.

### What could sink it

- **Averages are not dynamics.** A cohort moved by expected rates can drift from what individual shoots would do where growth feeds back (the hand-written closures of October ran 14 to 17% over on the oak). Keyed whole-number cohorts and hazards read at the cell reduce this; whether the trees then read right is unknown until grown.
- **The emergent rules may not draw a good oak.** Vigour-driven form is published and plausible, and no measurement here shows it yields a broad low-branched oak, a clear-boled beech and a dense spruce from sourced traits.
- **It is a rewrite of the grower's core**, with every digest and baseline changing.
- **A realised twig must agree with its cohort** closely enough that zooming shows no change in amount. Untested.

### What it reopens, for the owner

- STRATEGY.md's "one persistent botanical structure from trunk to leaf-bearing twig": the leafy shoots stop being stored state and become a function of the structure, realised on demand.
- The device grower, a sanctioned exception since October, is deleted.
- "No shortcut that changes the grown tree beyond its measured tolerance" (13 Oct): there is no second tree to stay within tolerance of; the cohort model is the tree.
- Every preset is re-derived on sourced traits; today's by-eye architecture rows and fitted values go.

### How it would be proven before it is built

Three proofs, each small, each able to stop the approach, in this order:

1. **The cohort law on paper and in a probe:** for the oak, spruce and beech, a cohort per bearing stretch moved by the species' own tables over a shoot's own life with hazards at the cell, run beside today's cycle as an instrument. Read: leaf area index, shoots a year and leaf per cell against published ranges and against the cycle, and time. Stop if it is no closer to real trees than the cycle and no cheaper by five times.
2. **The emergent structure in a probe:** vigour across orders, promotion by vigour, posture by performance and the height limit on the structural axes alone, for the oak at 40, 120 and 300 and the beech at 40, as stills for the owner beside fn-336's sheets. Stop if the owner does not find them better.
3. **A twig realised from its key against its cohort:** amounts within a stated share over a limb, the same twig across ten years, on one sheet at 5 cm.
