## The oak on the 11 Nov build (seeds 7, 1001, 1007, 1012)

- **40:** a young oak with a full crown on a stout stem. What is wrong: a litter of low branches lying on the ground round the base on every seed, and a trunk too thick for the tree (56 to 75 cm; twelve-seed median 78 cm against a real open-grown oak's 28 to 40).
- **80:** the best age. A broad spreading crown on a short bole; seed 7 a dome, seed 1001 a V of two leaders. The trunk is 1.1 to 2.2 m across, two to three times a real oak's.
- **120:** recognisably an old open oak in outline, but the limbs are long leaning poles with leaf at their ends; seed 7 throws bare whips out to one side; seeds 1007 and 1012 lean off their bases.
- **200:** not an oak. A single bare curving stem with a tuft on top on three seeds of four; seed 1007's stem runs along the ground before rising. Seed 7 is the least bad, a thick pole forking high.
- **300:** worse than 200. Sinuous bare stems with small tufts; seed 1007 loops on the ground.
- **Seed 7 (L6):** sound at 40, 80 and 120; at 200 and 300 the same bare pole as the other seeds. Its fault is the old tree's, not a fault of its own.

The measures agree with the eye: the lowest living limb's median climbs from 0.4 m at 40 to 16.5 m at 200 and 20.2 m at 300 on trees of 32 and 35 m; leaf area falls from 1,409 m² at 120 to 561 m² at 300; the trunk at 1.3 m thins from 2.02 m at 120 to 1.28 m at 300.

## 0.1.4, for scale of the claim "strictly better"

- 0.1.4's oak is a dense green shrub with no visible bole, the same shape on every seed. Today's oak at 40 to 120 is the better tree by a wide margin; at 200 and 300 0.1.4 has nothing to compare.

- 0.1.4's Laurelin and Telperion are drawn leafless by its headless example, so they cannot be compared from these stills.
