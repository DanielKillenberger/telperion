1. **The stop rule's reading.** As the goal writes it: the approach stops only if the cohorts are both no closer to real trees and no cheaper by five times. The paper arithmetic (at most 3.5 to 4.8 times on the oak's and 3.8 on the beech's branch wood, from cohorts alone, with shoot counts equal to the cycle's in expectation) predicts that both fail on those two species. The host's estimate in RETHINK-2026-11-10.md ("an order of magnitude at 40") was too high for the oak and the beech at this boundary and is corrected in the goal's log.

2. **A cohort is a count of whole lateral subtrees.** It is counted by its root lateral's age on the reference axis and its birth year, on the growth unit that bears it. What it contains (shoots by age, leaf, wood) is the expectation over its own years since birth from the same tables, with the cell's light in its survival. Its root's death sheds the whole of it after the root's `shedding`, so nothing it bears outlives it. This answers "shedding needs lineage" and "counted on the phytomer or a stretch": the stretch is the bearer's growth unit.

3. **Both births.** Buds a node sprouts and sleeping buds that wake, each by the tables (`dormant`, `delay`, `rate`), in expectation.

4. **Both hazards.** The cohort's survival takes the shade hazard at its cell's light, and its own carbon balance (its leaf's light against its wood's upkeep) takes the balance hazard as a lateral's does. Where the balance hazard never fires at the measured light, the result says so.

5. **Which leaf shades.** As today, the current year's leaf is what a cohort deposits, so the probe changes one thing at a time. The standing leaf by `leaf_years` is counted and reported beside it for R2's leaf area index. Whether older leaf should shade is a question for the presets' re-derivation, named in the result.

6. **Sizes.** A cohort's subtrees take the mean size, 1, since the vigour split is normalised so each age's mean unit stays whole. What the oak loses by that (its `shade_size` on twig and short) is reported from the replay.

7. **Section.** A cohort's root radius is found by its own ages' exponents and joins its bearer by the bearer's exponent, as the girth of a grown lateral does.

8. **Whole numbers by degree.** A count is its whole part plus one member at the presence of its fraction, as a zone's fractional `buds` already stands. No keyed rounding. The tree then changes by degree as a setting moves.

9. **The cell.** The lattice stays at 0.5 m. The boundary is read at 0.5 m, with 0.25 m and 1.0 m as thresholds only, to show its sensitivity.

10. **The boundary for this child.** Plain, by an age's expected reach against the threshold, as R1-LAW.md tables it. How a lateral near it passes by degree, and how a cohort member that would outgrow its cell is promoted, stay open for the build and are named in the result.
