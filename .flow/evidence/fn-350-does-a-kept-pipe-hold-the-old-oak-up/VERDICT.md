## What the measurement settles

- **Thinning wood is what lays the old oak down.** With the kept pipe at 0.5 or 1 the oak at 300 on seed 1001 stands 23 m on one stem and no longer loops to the ground, and the oak at 200 on seed 1012 is drawn where the fix branch refuses it. The trunk at 1.3 m no longer falls from 120 to 300 on any seed.
- **It costs nothing to grow:** 120.6, 122.0 and 121.7 s and 1.99 GiB at the three values.

## What it does not give: a tree to ship

- **Every tree from 80 on is worse to look at than today's at 0.5 and at 1.** The trunk is 2.6 to 5.4 m across where a real 300-year oak is about 1.5 to 2.5 m. The base is a flared disc with stepped rings above it, a pot under the tree. At 300 on seed 1012 the lower stem is an open, broken cylinder; on seed 1007 a cut drum with a stub.
- **The crown at 300 is still a small tuft,** and the drawn tree is 16 to 23 m where the grower holds 30 to 33 m. Keeping the pipe does not renew the crown.
- The young trees keep height and spread; only the trunk thickens (39% at 40, 84% at 80).

## Why the trunk is too thick (the host's reading, arithmetic not yet built)

The oak's pipe per node was fitted with the kept share at 0, so that the living crown alone makes the whole trunk. In a real oak the living pipes are the sapwood, a thin outer band, and the rest of the trunk is disused pipe. Fitting the whole trunk to the living pipes and then also keeping the disused ones counts the trunk several times over.

From this run's numbers: at 1 the trunk at 120 is 2.4 to 2.5 times today's in diameter on all three seeds. A pipe smaller by that factor squared (about 5.6 to 6.3 in section) would give today's trunk at 120 and, by the same scaling, 167, 216 and 207 cm at 300 on seeds 1001, 1007 and 1012, and about 33 cm at 40. Those are an old oak's girths. Whether limbs that thin hold up under sag at 40 and 80 is not known.

- The open cylinder at 300 on seed 1012.
