# fn-299 R1: the repro, the trace and a proposed rule

Run on 2026-10-20 at `4ddffda27` plus the R1 test. Two `telperion-heavy 8 --threads 2` admissions, CPU only, with no queue wait. Logs are `raw/r1-run.log` (the repro, the trace and the probe) and `raw/r1-trace-b.log` (residual frames under rule B). The scratch tests are kept as `raw/fn299_scratch.rs.scratch` and were never committed. The quantity throughout is `most_units(growth(families(a, b, t)).species)`.

**Verdict:** the trace confirms the suspected cause and adds one row to it, `continuation` at the ages where the oak stops. A rule that lets an age one end never reaches take the other end's settings brings oak to palm and palm to laurelin within their ends on every film frame. Walks whose ends both reach an age but only one lives it still leave their ends by 1 to 2 units. No single obvious rule removes that residue, so this goes back to the host. Nothing outside the test was changed.

## Proposed rule (rule B)

**For 0 < t < 1, an age one end never reaches takes the other end's settings, every row of it, before the rows mix. An age that both ends reach, or neither reaches, mixes as today.**

- An end "reaches" an age when a bud of that end is ever in it, entered or passed through. The search starts from the seed's PA 0, follows each apex's successor chain, and follows each lateral or dormant bud's PA with probability above 0. The probe's `reached` is that search; read at the ends, it shows the palm reaches ages 0 to 2 only and the oak reaches all 15.
- This answers the spec's question first. An age one end never reaches is not blended at all. Its rows are the other end's, and what fades over the walk is how often the age is entered: age 2's continuation (1 − t) and the laterals into the age, both of which already mix by value. The tree changes by degree.
- **Ends unchanged.** The swap applies only strictly between the ends, and `families` returns both ends unchanged. At t = 1 the swapped ages switch back to the palm's placeholders, a step in rows that no bud reaches. That is inferred, not measured. Task .3's end digests check the bytes.
- **Its effect on oak to palm (measured, `most_units`):**

| t | 0.5 | 0.9 | 0.97 |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| base | 3 | 7 | 12 |
| rule B | 2 | 2 | 2 |

  What changes in the drawn tree is unmeasured; that is task .3's stills. Inferred from the rows: from t = 0.9 on, age 2 lives 117 to 150 years, so no apex leaves it within 40 years, and those frames should barely move. At t = 0.5 age 2 lives 43 years, also past the tree's 40; any oak lateral that enters ages 3 to 14 keeps the oak's timing there, where the base shortened it.
- **Where it lives:** in `blend.rs`, with the reach search on `Species` next to `lived` and `successor`. One new pass over each end. No new row, no clamp on the count.

## What rule B leaves, and the tolerance (host's call)

Rule B does not touch an age both ends reach. Two mechanisms remain. Only the first is traced at its own frame. The second is read from one frame (birch to oak at 0.5). The worst counts at other frames (spruce to birch 3, birch to oak 4) were not traced to either.
1. **Pass-through slivers (traced).** These are ages one end passes through on the way to a later age, and the other end lives. Examples are beech ages 0, 1, 10 and 11, and oak age 5 on birch to oak. The blend has to start their lifespan from 0 at the passing end. Each such age entered then counts a whole unit at t = 0⁺. A continuous lifespan blend cannot avoid that. Only a new row, a chance that an age is skipped, or a count that weighs a unit by its share of a cycle would avoid it. Both of those are model changes and out of this spec's scope.
2. **Mixed outcomes (read, birch to oak t = 0.5 under rule B: 3).** Birch age 5 stops with a relay (continuation 0, `relay_ended` 1). The oak passes through age 5 (canonical continuation 1). Blended, age 5 may both stop with a relay and move on (continuation 0.5). `most_units` counts both outcomes, the age's unit plus the relay plus the next age's unit, which gives 3. Neither end has an age that does both.

Options for the host:
- **(a) Rule B, with a tolerance of ends' max + 2.** That is the measured worst case across the six walks under rule B (beech to spruce 4 and birch to oak 4 against 2). The first test rewrites `the_floor_counts_the_ages_a_cycle_chains` to keep its 4 and name the sliver case as the reason. The second keeps it unchanged with that reason stated. Every film frame then stays at or below 4, far under `FRAMES` (11).
- **(b) Rule B, held strictly ([min, max], tolerance 0) on walks with no age that one end passes through and the other lives, and at + 2 on the walks that have one.** It is stricter where rule B is exact, and needs a per-walk classification in the test.
- **(c) Rule B plus a further rule for pass-through ages.** No candidate stays continuous at the passing end; this needs a design decision beyond the blend.

Recommended: (a). It is the smallest rule that removes the defect the film hit (12 and 11 units). Its residue is bounded and named. The owner's "change by degree" holds, because nothing switches.
