## What it shows

- **The drawn wood agrees.** The curve's rings reach exactly as low as `mesh::build`'s surface on every tree. Nothing extra is drawn below ground on either path.
- **The difference is the bounding box.** On the fine path (backend `Gpu`, either delivery) `prepare` takes its bounds from `Curve::bounds()` (`preparation.rs:266`, `fine/resident.rs:330`) and the device's handoff (`handoff.wgsl:159-162`). Both are the box of every cluster's sphere, `centre ± reach`. At the trunk's foot that sphere reaches its whole radius below the base: 0.35 m on the beech at 40, 2.6 m on the oak at 80.
- **Not a device/CPU split.** `Delivery::Cpu` on the fine path gives the same box as the device. The date palm, which falls back off the fine path, gets the surface's bounds.
- **What reads the box:** the hero camera (`hero_pose` via `web.rs:266`), the shadow fit (`lib.rs:480`) and the figure's height (`generation/submit.rs:46`).

## For the host

The spheres are a deliberate cheap, conservative box (`Curve::bounds`: "the box round every ring, lobes included"). Making the fine path agree with `mesh::build` means a tighter box on both the CPU curve and the device handoff, for example each ring's own extent (radius times the root of one minus the tangent's component squared, lobes added). That changes `prepare`'s bounds, and with them framing, shadow fit and figure scale on every fine-path tree, though not a drawn byte. Per the dispatch, R1 stops here for the host's decision.

## Host decision (2026-10-09)

Tighten the box: build it from each ring's own extent on both sides, the CPU curve (`Curve::bounds()`) and the device handoff (`handoff.wgsl`). A ring's extent is its centre plus or minus its radius, lobes included. This stays a conservative bound on the drawn wood, which is what the doc comment already promises. It now agrees with `mesh::build` instead of hanging up to 2.6 m below the ground.

- **No drawn byte changes.** Framing, the shadow fit and the figure's height move toward the true tree, and that is the intent.
- **The extent comes from the existing pass.** Take it from the same pass that writes the rings, with no added pass, and measure the cost.
- **Proof:**
  - A test that the fine path's bounds equal `mesh::build`'s within fn-125's screen for every shipped species at 40 and 80.
  - The hero stills before and after, viewed, showing the framing.

Then R2, R3, R4, impl-review and `flowctl done`.

## Built as the host decided (2026-10-09)

**What changed:**
- **CPU curve.** `CurveCluster` carries `bounds`, the box round its rings: each ring's centre plus its radius (times the lobe factor) times its plane's extent on each axis, `sqrt(normal_k² + binormal_k²)`. That is the exact extent of a circle in the ring's plane, so the box stays conservative and is tight on round wood. `clusters` computes it in the loop that already walks every point for the centre. `Curve::bounds` unions the clusters' boxes in place of their spheres.
- **Device handoff.** `handoff.wgsl` `pack_clusters` folds the same box in its existing loop over the cluster's points (the sag loop), and the lobe factor rides in the config's spare word. No pass, buffer, binding or readback was added.
- **Reading the host's rule.** The decision says "centre plus or minus its radius". A sphere per ring would still hang the trunk's foot its radius below the ground, so I read "its own extent" as the ring's extent in its own plane, which is what agrees with `mesh::build`.
