Existing values can address the following components. These are directions to test, not demonstrated fixes.

| Difference | Existing rows and direction |
|---|---|
| Excessive or misplaced major branches | Increase `leaderInternode` or decrease `lateralsPerStation` on leaders; increase `lateralSpacing` on their branches. Raise plane `forkHeight` and, where needed, `crownBase`. **`lateralSpacing` does not space leader stations.** |
| Long, repetitive beech branches | Lower `lateralLengthRatio`; increase `raggedReach` for variation among first scaffold laterals. Increase twig `vigourVariation` for local length variation. The first two operate at different architectural levels. |
| Straight beech rods / winding plane branches | Modestly increase beech `crookedness`; reduce plane’s inherited 24°. Adjust `risePrimary`/`riseSecondary` for broad curvature. Increase beech `pitchVariation`; use negative `pitchByHeight` where upper departures should become more upright. |

Two narrower capability limitations are verified, although these photographs do not yet prove that either requires new code:

- **Repeated subdivision along a limb’s own course.** A horizontal or high-born limb cannot reliably undergo successive forks through the current absolute-height rule. Expressing that architecture requires division opportunities related to the limb’s own development or extent; local twig axes also currently lack codominant division.
- **Progressively shorter distal scaffold branches.** Deeper scaffold laterals all inherit the same fraction of their parent’s full length, without a departure-position term or independent length variation. Expressing a systematic distal shortening would require that relationship within the existing branching law.

The host’s proposal needs four corrections.

1. **Keep the plane as a capability probe, while deferring species acceptance.** Its inherited oak values make species resemblance an unfair acceptance test. Its exposed leaders and second-order arms remain useful evidence about shared generator behavior.

2. **Retain counts, but repair the measurements.** The beech’s appearance changed locally; its dominant visual defect survived. Counts are insufficient rather than irrelevant. R1 excludes codominant forks from lateral statistics, and R5’s tie-break explicitly selected the candidate with fewer limb forks. That selection works against the host’s current hypothesis. 3. **Hold the shell, and test whether it can be deleted.** Dormancy establishes compatibility, not usefulness. Also correct the spec’s claim that removing inner twigs leaves remaining scaffold limbs thicker: the pipe solve sums only nodes before `tree.crossover`; twig removal does not reallocate that scaffold pipe. 4. **Make the next measurement small and discriminating.** Trace a few clearly visible beech branch systems. Record daughter-to-daughter thickness, uninterrupted length between substantial divisions, diameter retained along each path, daughter length relative to the remaining parent extent, curvature and departure-angle variation. Include codominant continuations. Compare matched-camera bare renders first, then leaves, using existing values before declaring a gap.

My recommendation is a bounded direct-build comparison with the shell neutral, followed by a shell ablation on the best structural candidate. Keep `twigShell` only if it adds a distinct visual improvement after those corrections.
