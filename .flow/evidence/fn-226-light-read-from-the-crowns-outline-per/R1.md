# fn-226 task 1, R1: the lattice's cost by pass, and which effects come from which (worker, 2026-10-08)

Evidence only. The instrumentation (`r1-instrumentation.patch`) was applied for the runs and then reverse-applied, so `git diff -- crates/` is empty. It adds:
- `grow/stepped/probe.rs`;
- timers in `grow/stepped.rs`, `stepped/feedback.rs` and `light/field.rs`;
- `examples/r1.rs`.

With the patch applied, the four lit oaks' digests equal the unpatched build's (`raw/r1/digests-unpatched.txt`), so the probe times today's tree.

## How measured

- **Base and build:** `e193ea55` (F4 with contract A, the stepped grower and the exchange). `--profile ci`, `RAYON_NUM_THREADS=8`, this worktree's own `target/`. Ryzen 9 5950X, 32 threads.
- **The oaks:**
  - **Lit:** `oak()` under `Light { extinction: 0.5, sky: 0.5 }`.
  - **Unlit:** `Light::NEUTRAL`.
  - Both built by `grow` (the stepped executor, cut 1,024).
- **Load:** `r1-run.sh` waits for a one-minute load under 6 before each tree. Each row of `raw/r1/r1.jsonl` records its starting load (4.2 to 6.6).
- **Repeats:** lit and unlit are the best of three builds. Each switch is one build.
- **Timing:**
  - Each pass is wall time on the calling thread, summed over the cycles.
  - The items' rough layout was moved out of their growth into a second pass over the items. The tree is the same (digests above).
  - The lattice's box, deposits and sweep are timed apart inside `Field::on`.
- **Measures:** these follow OAK-LIGHT.md's definitions.
  - **Crown x / y:** the extent of the kept tips.
  - **Crown base:** live and major.
  - **Shed:** grown minus kept, as a share of grown. Grown comes from the executor's counts.

  The numbers differ from OAK-LIGHT.md's because the base has moved: F4 merged after `bd291391`.
- **Tables:** `r1-tables.py` (`raw/r1/tables.md`).

**The lattice itself is 11 to 13 % of the lit build.** Here the lattice means the spec's deposit and sample: the six shade columns, gather to write back. That is 0.07 s at 40 s1, 0.90 s at 80 s1 and 1.60 s at 80 s7.
- The sweep is 1 %.
- The deposit is most of it, 6 to 7 %, and runs on one thread.

**What else light costs.** None of this work is done for the unlit oak:
- **Re-lay** (assemble, today's full lay with sag, disperse): 16 to 27 %. It is the largest pass at 80 (1.9 s and 3.8 s). The seven re-lays at 80 lay 8.6M and 15.2M phytomers.
- **Allotment** (ii, iii): 16 to 18 %. It runs only where a rough layout is grown (`allots()`), so the unlit oak has none.
- **Rough layout** (coarse and items): 9 to 11 %. It exists only for light.
- **Balance** (i): 5 to 6 %. It has the same gate (`balances()`).
- **Stepping every cycle:**
  - **Why the lit oak's items step every cycle:** balance and allotment cross at each cycle's start, and light crosses at its end (`schedule.rs`, `reach`).
  - **The unlit oak instead:** its roots go over as buds and grow alone.
  - **What it costs:** the lit oak's growth, wake and handover take 0.8 to 1.5 s at 80. The whole unlit build takes 1.0 to 2.5 s.

**Lit against unlit:** the lit build is 5.1 to 5.6 times the unlit at 40 and 5.4 to 7.1 times at 80.

## Which effect comes from which pass

Each variant turns one pass or reader off. Everything else stays as in the lit oak:
- **flat:** every bud reads light 1, so there is no deposit, sweep or sample. The rough layout, re-lay, balance and allotment still run.
- **norelay:** no re-lay. Light is read on the rough layout.
- **noallot:** `shade_size` 0 on every PA, so the allotment does not run.
- **nobalance:** `balance_hazard` 0.
- **nohazard:** `shade_hazard` 0.
- **nogirth:** `leaf_girth` 0.

What it shows:

**1. Crown width comes from the allotment reading differences in light.**

| Oak 80, crown x / y (m) | lit | noallot | unlit | flat |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| s1 | 24.0 / 21.3 | 17.8 / 18.0 | 18.0 / 17.9 | 19.3 / 17.2 |
| s7 | 27.8 / 30.6 | 17.8 / 19.6 | 17.8 / 19.7 | 22.9 / 23.9 |

- **noallot** is as narrow as unlit.
- **flat** keeps the allotment but gives every bud the same light, and it is narrow too.
- **Height** follows the same pattern: lit 22.5 and 26.8 m, noallot 20.5 and 23.0 m, unlit 20.0 and 22.6 m.

**2. The clear trunk comes from the balance.**
- At 80, nobalance keeps live low shoots on the stem, as unlit does: its live crown base is 0.36 and 1.44 m, against 4.70 and 4.66 m lit.
- Every other switch keeps the clear bole.
- nobalance is also wider: 27.7 / 24.2 and 32.9 / 33.0 m.

**3. Openness comes from the shade hazard: fewer phytomers kept, more shed.**
- **Grown:** nohazard grows 1.28 and 1.33 times the lit oak's phytomers at 80, and 1.12 to 1.24 times at 40.
- **Kept:** nohazard keeps 1.11 to 1.28 times as many. No other reader moves kept by more than 11 %.
- **Shed share:** it moves little in any row (lit 79.1 and 76.2 %, nohazard 80.2 and 80.1 %). Light changes how much is grown, not the share that is shed.

**4. The re-lay changes little.**
- **Width:** norelay is within 0 to 5 % of lit (80 s7: 28.3 / 32.1 against 27.8 / 30.6 m).
- **Kept and trunk:** it keeps 4 to 5 % fewer phytomers at 80 and keeps the clear trunk.
- **Cost:** it builds 18 to 29 % faster at 80.
- The re-lay is the most expensive light pass and the one with the least effect on these measures. Stills were not taken.

**5. Leaf girth** changes none of these measures. It thickens the wood; it does not change the form.

**6. The lattice is not where light's cost lies.** flat costs as much as lit (7.9 s and 17.1 s at 80): everything except the lattice still runs, and the tree grows more.

## What this means for R2 and R3 (for the host)

- **The most a per-bud read can save is the lattice's 11 to 13 %.**
- **The per-cycle synchronisation stays.** For any species that reads light, balance (i) and allotment (ii, iii) are tree-wide reductions at the start of every cycle, whatever reads the light.
- **The larger costs lie elsewhere:**
  - the re-lay, at 16 to 27 %, with little visible effect here;
  - the allotment, at 16 to 18 %, which makes the crown's width.
