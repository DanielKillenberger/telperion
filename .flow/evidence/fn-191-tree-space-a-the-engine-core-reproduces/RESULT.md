# fn-191 result: the engine core reproduces GreenLab

The engine core is the crate `crates/telperion-space`. It is not wired into the pipeline. Its structures match GreenLab's closed-form counts exactly, and they match Letort's simulators: exactly on deterministic runs, and in distribution on stochastic ones. Everything below was measured on 2026-10-03 on the desk (AMD Ryzen 9 5950X).

## What the engine holds (R1)

- **Buds with a physiological age (PA).** Each PA is one state on the species' reference axis (`Species.states`, youngest first). A state gives the apex its lifespan in growth units, its next PA (the oriented automaton; a PA never grows younger), its viability per cycle, its growth unit, its shedding delay and its geometry.
- **Growth units of phytomers.** A growth unit is a list of zones, base to top. Each zone draws its node count, uniform or Poisson, and each of its 1 to 6 buds per node draws a lateral PA. The zone sets a lateral's PA by its position, as GreenLab's micro-states do. Within a zone the nodes stand acrotonically: bare nodes at the base and the youngest lateral PA on top.
- **Death and pruning.** An apex survives each cycle with its PA's viability. A lateral axis whose subtree has had no living apex for longer than its PA's shedding delay is shed, together with everything it bears.
- **Minimal geometry.** Axes are straight. Internode lengths, insertion angles and divergence come per PA.
- **Errors.** Every input the engine cannot draw is refused with its path, for example `states[1].zones[0].lateral[0]`. Three conditions are errors in their own right: a seed that dies before it grows (`Collapsed`), a tree past its phytomer budget, and wood below the ground. All are tested in `tests/refusals.rs`.

## Closed form (R2)

`expected_counts` is GreenLab's structural factorisation. A bud's substructure depends only on its PA and age, so each is computed once. Under deterministic laws it gives the counts themselves; under stochastic laws it gives their expectation, viability included.

`tests/closed_form.rs` checks it four ways:
- On three parameter sets beyond the simulators' reach (position-set lateral PA in zones, whorls of three with a self-renewing apex, reiteration), the engine's counts per PA and cycle equal the formula exactly.
- The formula equals the simulators' three deterministic trees.
- It matches one count worked by hand.
- `tests/death.rs` checks the expectation, with mortal apices, against the engine's mean over 4,000 seeds.

## The oracle (R3)

The oracles are Letort's two simulators. `PlantStructureFactoryStochastic` at `9ac7a9fd` is the alternate one; `PlantStructureFactoryStochastic_Acer` at `7f30aca4` is the opposite one, with two independent buds per node. Each was run unchanged in headless Chromium by `scripts/greenlab-oracle.mjs`. The script pins each simulator by commit and sha256 and drives its own `makeRNG` and `buildAxis`. The repositories carry no licence, so they are cloned outside the repository and never copied.

The script ran eight parameter sets: the simulators' defaults under both count laws, on both simulators; a four-PA set with a perpetual PA 2 and a PA 3 that ages into PA 4; and three deterministic sets. Each stochastic set ran 4,000 seeds. The record is `crates/telperion-space/tests/fixtures/greenlab-oracle.json`, which holds each count's mean, variance and fourth moment per PA and cycle, plus each deterministic tree's counts and the hash of its canonical structure string.

`tests/oracle.rs`:
- **Deterministic runs.** The three trees match the simulators exactly: every count, and the canonical structure string (length and FNV-1a hash).
- **Stochastic runs.** Our 4,000 seeds against the oracle's 4,000, over 368 comparisons of mean and variance per PA, per cycle and per total. The worst difference is 3.81 standard errors (opposite-poisson, PA 3, cycle 4, variance); the bar is 4.5. Scaling every lateral probability by 1.03 fails it at 9.71.
- **The bridge.** The closed-form expectation is within 4.5 standard errors of the oracle's mean in every cell.

The oracle ran on the first attempt. None of the three recorded attempts the wrong-path stop allows was used.

## The sheet and the time per structure (R5)

`raw/sheet/sheet.png` sets each parameter set's simulator drawings at seeds 1 and 2 beside ours at seeds 1 and 2. Ours are drawn from our own geometry, distichous and therefore planar, seen from the side. It is regenerated by:

    cargo run --release -p telperion-space --example sheet -- crates/telperion-space/tests/fixtures/greenlab-oracle.json <raw>/ours
    node scripts/greenlab-oracle.mjs sheet <clones> crates/telperion-space/tests/fixtures/greenlab-oracle.json <raw>/ours <raw>/sheet

On the deterministic rows the worker read the trees as the same: the same axes, the same PA transitions (green PA 2 turning red PA 3 at the same rank) and the same branching per growth unit. Our internodes and angles are our own, so the lengths differ. The simulator also fades axes that have stopped growing and we do not, which is a drawing choice. The host has not yet viewed the sheet.

Time per structure, as a mean over the seeds each side ran. Ours is the release build over 2,000 seeds: growth, shedding and geometry, plus the count table, its totals and dropping the tree. The simulator's is its `makeRNG` and `buildAxis` in Chromium's V8, plus our walk of its tree into a count table.

## Decisions

- **The node-count law is uniform or Poisson.** Both simulators offer both, and R3 compares "for the same parameters". It is a choice between distributions, not between ways of building. Phase B, whose remit is discrete botany as settings, may fold it into one continuous family.
- **Acrotony within a zone is fixed.** It is the simulators' order. Basitony is expressed by the order of zones; a continuous tonality setting is left to the species phases.
- **Shedding is decided at the tree's age.** A shed subtree has no living apex, so nothing on it could have grown later, and deciding at the end is exact.
- **The crate rides in CI's small-crate job** (`rust-wasm` suite key and packages) rather than as a suite of its own.
