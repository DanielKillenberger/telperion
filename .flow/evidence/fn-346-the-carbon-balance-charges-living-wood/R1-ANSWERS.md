## What the note changes

The charge as the host wrote it (K × path per tip, K = 1 / 34.8 m) is refuted as written: it passes the oak's income at 45 and is 2.4 times income at 119. The cause is the host's: the charge was written per tip, while a tip's light is already its size times the light it stands in (`base_scale × running × lattice light`). A small tip was charged a whole pipe and credited a small tip's light.

## The rule, restated

A living tip's upkeep is its size times its path over the species' path limit:

    cost of a tip = base_scale × running × L / L_max
    light of a tip = base_scale × running × lattice light      (as today)

- A tip therefore earns its cost exactly where the light it stands in is at least L / L_max. Near the root any lit tip pays; at L_max only a fully lit one does. The light a shoot needs rises with the wood that serves it, and that is the whole rule.
- A subtree's cost is the sum over its living tips (reading (a): the pipes are charged over the whole path to the root, since under (b) nothing pays for the trunk). Wood that has died adds nothing. Today's per-metre upkeep is not added beside it: the pipes are the wood.
- `L` is the path along the wood from the root collar to the tip, as the census carries it.
- `L_max` is the species' potential height, a measured trait (oak 34.8 m; the others as fn-345 sourced them). Reading it against path and as the break-even of a fully lit tip is this model's statement and is listed as an estimate (questions 3 and 8). Path runs 12 to 30% over height, so the tree's height comes out below the species' potential; an open-grown tree stands below a forest one.
- Light rising with path (question 5) is taken as found: the rule compares each tip's own light with its own cost and needs no premise about it.
- The balance, its memory, the hazard, `kept`, tolerance and every preset value other than `upkeep` stay as they are. Girth is not touched (question 7).
- Dormant where `L_max` is unset: such a species runs today's charge with dead wood not charged.

## The main line (question 4)

Two variants, since this is the open point:

- **P1:** the rule above and nothing else. The seed and what carries it on are untested, as today.
- **P2:** P1, and an axis that continues or relays its bearer is tested on its own subtree as a lateral is. Only the seed's own axis is never shed. So the part of the tree above any point on the main line is shed or faded by the same balance as a limb.
