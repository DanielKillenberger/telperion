## 1. Plant architecture

### 1.1 What separates the Hallé–Oldeman models

An architectural model is "the fundamental growth programme on which the entire architecture is established" [BC07, read]. Hallé and Oldeman (1970) and Hallé, Oldeman and Tomlinson (1978, "HOT") reduced tree form to **23 models**. Each model is one combination of four groups of features [BC07, read; T83, read]:

1. **Growth pattern:** determinate or indeterminate; rhythmic (endogenous periodic stops, giving growth units) or continuous.
2. **Branching pattern:**
   - terminal, lateral or none;
   - **monopodial** (the terminal bud continues the axis) or **sympodial** (the axis ends by flowering, abortion or parenchymatization, and a lateral takes over);
   - **rhythmic** (tiers), **continuous** (every node) or **diffuse** (irregular);
   - **immediate** (sylleptic) or **delayed** (proleptic).
3. **Axis differentiation:**
   - **Orthotropic** axes are erect, radially symmetric and branch in all directions.
   - **Plagiotropic** axes are horizontal to slanted, often bilaterally symmetric (leaves and branches in one plane), and given to light capture and reproduction.
   - A single axis can be **mixed**: an orthotropic base and a plagiotropic tip, or the reverse.
   - Orthotropy is a "syndrome" of features, not only a direction [BC07, read].
4. **Flowering:** lateral or terminal. Terminal flowering ends an axis and forces sympodial growth.

**Models that matter for the catalogue** [BC07 Fig. 16, read; PR00, read; CIR, secondary]:

- **Corner:** one unbranched axis with lateral inflorescences.
- **Rauh:** rhythmic growth and branching; every axis is monopodial and orthotropic, and branches are morphogenetically like the trunk; flowering is lateral.
- **Massart:** a monopodial, rhythmic orthotropic trunk with plagiotropic branches in tiers or whorls.
- **Troll:** every axis starts plagiotropic. Height comes from the basal part of each module becoming secondarily erect. The trunk is a sympodial stack of such modules, with a renewal shoot at the bend of the parent and the distal part left as a branch.
- **Champagnat:** the reverse of Troll; axes start orthotropic and droop, and renewal shoots arise on the upper side.
- **Leeuwenberg:** sympodial; each orthotropic, determinate unit ends in flowering.

**Concepts from Barthélémy & Caraglio 2007** [BC07, read]:

- **Axis categories and the architectural unit.**
  - A species' architecture sorts into a small, finite number of axis categories, typically trunk, branch, branchlet and short shoot, and "no more than five or six" even in Cupressaceae. Their table of features is the species' architectural unit.
  - Categories need **not** coincide with branching order (Fig. 18): in *Platanus* and *Acer* a category recurs at several orders.
  - "The higher the branching order of an axis, the higher its degree of differentiation."
- **Physiological age.**
  - A meristem's differentiation state, read *a posteriori* from what it builds. Short, flowering, short-lived axes are "physiologically old" whenever they appear; vigorous main axes are "physiologically young".
  - Each entity has three ages: ontogenetic (the stage of the whole plant), chronological, and physiological. Physiological age depends on position and stage and is modulated by the environment.
  - Under suppression, the same species expresses the same sequence only partly (Fig. 33, case B).
- **Morphogenetic gradients:**
  - base effect: vigour rises through establishment;
  - drift: decline with ageing;
  - **acrotony, mesotony, basitony:** lateral development is favoured at the top, middle or base of a growth unit or shoot;
  - **epitony, amphitony, hypotony:** the upper, lateral or lower side of a slanted parent is favoured. Epitony is typical of the Champagnat, Troll and Mangenot models.
  - Position and vigour gradients are separate traits, with "no automatic correlation".
- **Reiteration.** A meristem repeats the whole or part of the architectural unit.
  - **Automatic (sequential)** reiteration is part of normal crown construction past a differentiation threshold. Mature broadleaf crowns are mostly built of reiterated complexes.
  - **Adaptive** reiteration answers more resources; **traumatic** reiteration answers damage.
  - Tomlinson: the mature crown shape comes from "architecture (deterministic) and reiteration (opportunistic)" [T83, read].
- **Ontogeny.** Models describe a sequence of architectures through life, not a snapshot. Mature trees often hide the model: in mature oaks the Rauh "blueprint" is "hardly recognisable" [K19, read].

## 2. Beech in detail

**Shoot types** [DTT86, read; LET, read; INN98, abstract and snippet]. Young *F. sylvatica* makes two kinds of annual shoot:

- **Short shoots ("exploitation"):**
  - 3 to 5 short internodes;
  - lateral buds "barely sketched, which never develop", so they **never branch**;
  - they carry **77% of the foliar area** (Renard 1971).
- **Long shoots ("exploration"):**
  - 6 to 10 internodes of variable length, with functional lateral buds;
  - they carry 63% of shoot dry weight;
  - only long shoots can be polycyclic (several flushes in one year).
- **Light moves the mix:** the share of exploration shoots is 36% in shade, 56% in half-light and 73% in full light. Height triples from shade to light [DTT86, read].
- **Mature trees:**
  - Innes reviews Roloff's four crown phases: exploration, degeneration, stagnation and resignation.
  - A search snippet of the paywalled text says short shoots are "generally <5 cm long" and carry "about 75 per cent of the foliage" [INN98, snippet only, unverified in full text].
  - All leaves are preformed in the winter bud [HAN11, read].
  - In a mature 30 to 35 m stand, sampled shoots carried **3.2 ± 0.2 leaves** non-fruiting and **4.7 ± 0.2** fruiting [HAN11, read].

**Monopodial and sympodial development** [DTT86, read]. Seedlings run in four modes: monopodial-straight, sympodial-straight (apical dominance transferred to a lateral), monopodial-forked and sympodial-forked. On average **93 to 95% of seedling years** were monopodial-straight. Forks arise only from exploration shoots. Polycyclism becomes common on rich sites.

**Troll's model in *Fagus*** [M98, read, *F. grandifolia*; same genus, same model per HOT]:

- **Seedling:** an orthotropic monopodium whose tip tilts. Phyllotaxy becomes alternate-distichous, and the first lateral is plagiotropic.
- **Modules:** each module is several growth units with an erect base and a plagiotropic tip.
  - The next module arises from a bud in the curvature zone (the relay), and apical dominance passes to it.
  - The module's head aborts or becomes a branch.
- **Young tree, about 13 m:** five axis categories (A1 to A5).
  - A1, the stem, is a mixed sympodium, orthotropic along almost all its length.
  - **A2 to A5 are plagiotropic monopodia with distichous phyllotaxy.**
  - Each A2 is a system of four categories "arranged in a single horizontal plane", dorsiventral and bilateral.
  - A5 are "short ageotropic shoots with very closely spaced leaves".
  - Low A2s are drooping and senescent; higher ones are oblique and thin.
- **Mature tree, about 20 m:** "at the top of the stem the branches become large and oblique, and a fork is created". The crown becomes "a succession of such reiterates, the most peripheral being the shortest", with only three categories. Height growth of the single stem stops at that fork.
- *F. sylvatica* specifically alternates orthotropic and plagiotropic phases, with returns to orthotropy (Thiébaut 1982, cited in [M98]).
- The CIRAD lecture describes beech the same way, and adds that in shade the axes cannot straighten and form a flat layer [CIR].

**Position within a shoot** [N98, abstract; LET, read]:

- Nicolini finds three gradients in 7-year-old forest beech:
  - establishment: node number, shoot length and branching rise year on year;
  - **primary acrotony** (Rauh): the highest laterals on the annual shoot form and grow most;
  - a decline of growth and branching on the lowest branches, which "announces their … disappearance by natural pruning".
- **GreenLab's calibrated beech** encodes the acrotonic sequence as zones along a long ramified growth unit (PA 2), base to tip:
  - Z²⁰: 3 metamers, no laterals;
  - Z²⁴: 3 metamers bearing short shoots (PA 4);
  - Z²³: 2 metamers bearing long shoots that carry only short shoots (PA 3);
  - Z²²: 1 metamer bearing a ramified long shoot (PA 2).
  - That is at most 9 metamers. Counts per zone scale with the plant's supply-to-demand ratio Q/D (Fig. 2) [LET, read].
- These are understorey trees, and calibrated values, not field statistics.

**Angles, crown base, pruning:**

- Terrestrial laser scans of 100 beech in pure and mixed stands found mean **branch angle 30.7° in pure stands and 54.1° in beech–pine mixtures**, and crown ratio 50.0% against 71.5% [RAIS21, abstract]. The angle's reference (vertical or stem) is not given in the abstract.
- In young stands, branch insertion angle depends mainly on branch cord length and relative position. Beech branches die late but shed fast, the opposite of oak [KINT10, abstract].
- Per-category angles for *F. sylvatica* were **not found**. [M98] gives categories, not degrees.
- Laterals per long shoot in mature crowns, long-shoot length distributions and internode lengths were **not found** in readable sources. Dupré's internode counts are for seedlings.

**What this means for the beech the owner sees.** A broad crown of many forked, oblique reiterated limbs. Each limb carries flat, two-ranked branch systems. Leaves sit mostly on short shoots of a few centimetres. Long shoots branch only near their tips (acrotony), so branching is shallow and spray-like rather than long deep chains. The low branches die and shed, which clears the bole.

## 3. Computational models

Positional functions set attributes along an axis. | The **rules themselves** (discrete apex types), plus positional curves. **Takeaways for fn-188:**

- In what I read, only physiology-first models (AMAP, GreenLab) encode **axis differentiation** as a parameter (physiological age) that spans species. Only competition models (Pałubicki, Runions) make density and crown form emergent.
- Stava and Li get breadth with **many direct parameters**, or with **two modes in sequence**.
- Makowski gets breadth with **discrete prototypes**.

## 4. Synthesis: one mechanism whose regions are architectural models

### 4.1 What the probe has and lacks (R3-PROBE3 to R3-PROBE8, ASTRA-TEXTURE-REVIEW, ASTRA-BARE-TARGETS)

| Ingredient (source) | Probe today | Gap seen on the beech |
|---|---|---|
| Competition for space or light, BH allocation, pipe radii [PAL09] | Space markers (binary Q); λ, α; leaf-pipe radii (exponent 2.9); bud retirement | At λ 0.5 every winning bud receives v = 2.0, so vigour is uniform (R3-PROBE8). With binary Q there is nothing to differentiate shoots. |
| **Axis categories / physiological age** [BC07; AMS08; GL21] | None. Every bud is the same kind; the scaffold rules stand in for the trunk and leaders. | No difference between upright leaders and spreading branches. "Fine structure as long deep chains": runs p50 0.5 m and 4 to 13 generations, against 1 to 3 shipped. |
| Short against long shoots [DTT86; LET] | R8 shoot form by vigour (sigmoid L) shortens internodes and suppresses laterals; inert at λ 0.5 | Short shoots should never branch and should hold most leaves. The probe puts 75% of leaves on branch wood. |
| **Positional lateral development (acrotony)** [N98; LET; BC07] | Every metamer bears one bud with equal chance | Laterals spread along whole shoots, which gives the comb; beech favours the distal laterals of each annual shoot. |
| Two-ranked, planar branch systems [M98] | R8 blend D: alternating sides about the horizontal side vector | Little effect on its own (R3-PROBE8). M98 describes whole A2 systems in one plane, not single shoots. |
| Plagiotropy and **reorientation over time** (Troll) [PR00; PAL09] | R7: one continuous plagiotropy parameter p; departure angle | No secondary straightening of module bases and no relay at the bend. PAL09 names this gap explicitly. |
| Reiteration and forking [M98; BC07] | Fork from the scaffold rules (G2) | The mature beech crown is forking reiterates; the probe grows one tree of laterals. |
| Self-pruning and clear bole [N98; PAL09; KINT10] | Takenaka shed tested once (80% shed with binary Q); markers fill to the crown base | Clear bole 0.04 to 0.07 H against about 0.20 H in the reference (ASTRA-BARE-TARGETS). |
| Light rather than space as the signal | One row (light × space): 2.1x the time | Graded Q is what gives vigour differences, shedding and drift. |

### 4.2 What a single mechanism would need

The literature points to a bud-level growth law whose behaviour is indexed by a **continuous physiological age φ** (AmapSim's reference axis, made continuous), with competition supplying vigour. Ingredients:

1. **State per bud:** φ, and v, the received resource from competition.
2. **Per-φ rule curves** (continuous functions of φ, not tables per category):
   - shoot size: metamers per shoot and internode length (short shoots at high φ);
   - lateral development along the shoot: an acrotony, mesotony or basitony profile, and the φ given to a lateral at each position (as GreenLab zones do);
   - direction: orthotropy against plagiotropy, the branching plane (phyllotaxis from spiral to distichous), and tropism over time, which for Troll and Champagnat includes secondary reorientation;
   - fate: monopodial continuation or terminal abortion/flowering (sympodial); a determinacy probability as in Makowski's D.
3. **Vigour couples to φ:** high v holds or lowers φ (the axis stays young), low v raises it. This is the "drift" and the light effect in DTT86 (36% exploration shoots in shade, 73% in light), and GreenLab's Q/D modulation.
4. **Allocation:** BH or priority, with graded light or space exposure so v varies (binary Q cancels at λ 0.5).
5. **Shedding** from the same light ÷ size budget, or from φ reaching a terminal value, with radius memory [PAL09].
6. **Reiteration:** a bud released by high v (or by the loss of its parent's leader) resets φ toward young. This is one rule covering automatic forking and adaptive reiteration.

**Under this framing the four catalogue models would be regions** (my reading; not shown in any source):

| Model | Region of the parameter space |
|---|---|
| Corner | Branching probability 0 at all φ. |
| Rauh | Orthotropy high at all φ, monopodial, acrotonic. |
| Massart | Orthotropy falls steeply with φ; laterals in rhythmic tiers. |
| Troll | Plagiotropy at all φ, with time-dependent straightening of module bases, and relays at the bend. |

Whether a smooth path between those regions avoids jumps (for example, Rauh to Troll) is untested.

### 4.3 Options (trade-offs, no pick)

- **A. Pałubicki plus physiological age.** Keep the probe's competition and add φ as one continuous state with rule curves.
  - Pro: one pipeline from trunk to twig; scaffold rules could be deleted; emergent density stays.
  - Con: the most new parameters, as curves. Troll needs reorientation, which is new. Cost grows with node count; foliage meshing already dominates (91%, ASTRA-TEXTURE-REVIEW).
- **B. GreenLab-style zones with competition-modulated counts.** Per-φ zone sequences; counts scale with local v instead of global Q/D.
  - Pro: calibrated beech topology exists [LET]; structural factorization could make it fast.
  - Con: rounding steps counts unless they are made fractional or probabilistic. No spatial competition unless markers or light are added.
- **C. Bi-modal (Li 2021).** Competition for structure, then a phenomenological law for the fine structure.
  - Pro: proven on seven species.
  - Con: it reinstates a seam between two laws, which is the assumption fn-188 set out to question.
- **D. Module library (Makowski 2019).** Grown structure from vigour, with fine structure instanced from prototypes per φ.
  - Pro: fastest, and ecosystem-scale.
  - Con: prototype choice is a discrete switch (Voronoi), which conflicts with "no parameter is a switch" unless the prototypes are blended. Fine structure does not respond to space.
- **E. Smallest step on the probe.** Add only acrotonic lateral development, short shoots that cannot branch, and graded light Q, then measure on the beech.
  - Pro: tests the three gaps Astra named, at low cost.
  - Con: says nothing yet about Massart, Corner or Troll straightening.

Open questions the sources do not answer: mature-beech laterals per long shoot and shoot-length percentiles; per-category branch angles for *F. sylvatica*; the London plane's HOT model name; the birch's model at species level.
