# The release film: through species space, trees growing and shrinking

## Goal & Context

The owner (2026-10-13): "i'd want to do the video we conceptualized at some point going through space and growing/shrinking trees. This should be much more efficient to do now right?"; "we can do it with release and embed it into the repos readme while we're at it". The teaser session (2026-10-09) drove `headless --walk` through species and ages and found fn-250 and fn-251. With fn-270 a tree grows once and each frame settles it at a fractional age (leaves and shoots unfolding, R5/R5a), so the age axis no longer regrows per frame; a step through species space is still one device build per frame (0.07-0.5 s warm, about 1.2 s for the Two Trees, fn-237 R0a). Growth is the product (AGENTS.md).

## Requirements

- **R1 (the path):** one declared path of segments, each either a growth (one species, age a0 -> a1, settled at fractional ages through one GrowthState) or a walk (species A -> B at one age, fn-250/251's walk); shrinking is a growth segment played in reverse; the shipped species and the Two Trees appear; the camera path is part of the declaration. The path is a value file read by the tool, not code.
- **R1a (the script, owner 2026-10-13: "that sounds good but i think it should be shorter/tighter yes"):** about 35 s, one continuous shot, the camera orbiting slowly and pulling back as the crown widens, no sound; the corner map of R1b. (1) 0-7 s: a beech grows from seed to 40; (2) 7-10 s: beech -> Norway spruce at 40; (3) 10-13 s: spruce -> silver birch at 40; (4) 13-16 s: birch -> Oregon white oak at 40; (5) 16-20 s: oak -> date palm at 40 (reordered by the host, 2026-10-14: fn-251 measured a dip through oak <-> spruce, fn-273; every step of the path must meet fn-251's oracle); (6) 20-23 s: the palm shrinks to a seedling (growth reversed); (7) 23-24 s: palm seedling -> Laurelin seedling, both at age 1 (fn-251: every frame passes there); (8) 24-31 s: Laurelin grows to 80; (9) 31-35 s: Laurelin -> Telperion at 80, gold to silver, flowers coming in, a one-second hold and a short closing line ("Every frame grown live by the engine"). Timings are a starting point the owner may retime after seeing a cut. Growth segments are drawn at whole years only, where the tree stands at full leaf, and crossfaded between years, never settled within a year (host ruling, 2026-10-14, on the owner's verdict on fn-270's four-draws-a-year grow-in: "leaves flickering in and out"); this drops the beech's "leaves unfolding each spring". Reference: fn-270's `raw/t5/whole/oregon-white-oak-whole-years.mp4` (6 years a second, crossfaded to 30 fps).
- **R1b (the map, owner 2026-10-13: "that sounds cool add it"):** a small corner map of species space. Each shipped species is a point: its catalogue rows in their natural scales (fn-251 R1b: log for magnitudes, log-odds for probabilities, linear for additive rows), each row divided by its spread across the species, age excluded. The map is the linear projection onto their first two principal components, so every walk stays a straight segment; each axis is labelled by code from its largest-loading rows, with the share of variance the two axes hold stated in the README text. The overlay draws the labelled species dots, a faint trail of the path so far, the current point and an age counter, flat 2D composited over the frame; the tree renderer is untouched. The projection is computed by code from the catalogue and tested (a walk's frames project onto the segment between its ends; t = 0 and 1 land on the ends' dots).
- **R2 (the tool):** a headless film command renders the path on the device at a stated resolution and frame rate, through the one pipeline and the shipped renderer (no film-only drawing), and assembles the video; per-segment cost (frames, build and settle time, peak memory) reported. One heavy run at a time.
- **R3 (fidelity in motion):** no pop between frames in growth segments (R5a's blend) and no frame over its ends' size in walks (fn-251's oracle); a defect found here is fixed by its own spec, never hidden by the camera or the cut.
- **R4 (the README):** the film is embedded in the repository README, the form that renders inline on GitHub found and checked (unknown today: a committed file against an uploaded asset), within the repository's size budget, with a still as fallback; the README's text around it says what the viewer is seeing in a sentence or two.
- **R5:** the owner's verdict on the film.
