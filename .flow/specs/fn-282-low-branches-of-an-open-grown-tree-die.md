# Low branches of an open-grown tree die as the crown above shades them

## Goal & Context

fn-252 (2026-10-14): the silver birch's lowest passing branches stay alive at 0.6-0.8 m from age 5 to 30, and their curtains sag onto the ground at 20-30. They read 0.85-0.98 of full light because they stand out from under the crown, so the shade hazard barely acts; and a branch lives while what it bears lives ("Life is unbounded where a lateral bears its own age", shed.rs), so a strand that keeps forking keeps its passing branch forever. A general rule tried in fn-252 (R2a: an age dies at its stated life) would have removed 9-33 % of the oak, beech and ordinary tree and was withdrawn. The owner (2026-10-14, "ok"): no birch-only value chosen for its effect; a general spec after the release. "but are we making up a rule for a specific species? that'd break our design rule no?"

## Requirements

- **R1 (sources and measurement first):** crown lift in open-grown trees from the literature (crown base height or live crown ratio at stated ages, for silver birch and every shipped species where found), checked against the texts; our crown base per species at 5-80 against it; the light our lowest branches read against what a branch under a crown receives (light model check: is self-shading from the crown above underestimated for low wood?).
- **R2 (the host designs, from R1):** the general mechanism (the light model's view of low wood, a branch's carbon balance, or the shedding law's lateral rule), by degree, one rule for every species, no species branch; species differ only by sourced values.
- **R3:** crown bases within the sources' ranges where sourced; every species' other measures within tolerance; stills viewed; cost measured; gate, npm, review; the owner's verdict.
