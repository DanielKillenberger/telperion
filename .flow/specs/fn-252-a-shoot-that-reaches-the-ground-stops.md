# A shoot that reaches the ground stops there

## Goal & Context

The owner, looking at the birch in the harness (2026-10-09, screenshot `.flow/evidence/fn-198-tree-space-f-the-engine-in-production/owner-birch-ground.png`): "lol what happend to this birch? i guess we accidentally invented roots? looks awesome but not like a birch". Hanging long shoots reach the ground and keep growing along it in loops, reading as roots. The cause is checked in code: the lay's resting rule (`crates/telperion-space/src/geometry/lay.rs:348`, "Resting: wood a step would take below the ground stays on it") clamps a non-trunk step at the ground and turns the shoot along it, so it grows on across the soil. Botanically a shoot meeting the ground does not grow on along it: its apex is abraded, shaded and grazed, and the strand ends at or just above the ground (the skirt line of a weeping birch). The same strands also read largely bare in their lower length on the screenshot; why is to be measured. This revisits fn-200's design ("The ground supports. Wood that bends down onto the ground rests on it and runs along it ... open-grown spruces lay their skirts on the ground and layer there"), which stays right for a sagging spruce skirt; what it did not foresee is a shoot that keeps extending along the ground for metres. Host (2026-10-09): release blocker (the birch is a release species).

## Requirements

- **R1 (measure first):** for the birch at 40 and 80 (seeds 1, 7, 42) and every other shipped species, how many axes and how much wood rest on the ground today, and at what age they reach it; whether both executors apply the resting rule (CPU `geometry/lay.rs`, the device's lay); why the lower hanging strands carry few leaves (their wood age against `leaf_years`, shedding, light), checked against the code.
- **R2 (the rule, by degree):** a per-age `ground_hazard` row (0 neutral: today's resting and running along the ground, fn-200's spruce skirt unchanged) sets how an apex that reaches the ground fares: its survival each cycle in contact falls with the row, by degree, through the existing making/abortion draws (no switch, no species branch), so at high values a strand ends at the ground and at low values a skirt rests and creeps. Applied in the one lay on both executors. Each species' value is sourced first: whether its shoots that reach the ground creep along it (and layer) or end there. The owner (2026-10-09) likes the ground-running growth and wants it kept as a feature for trees that do it, and is unsure whether silver birch does ("or maybe birches do do this"); dwarf birch (Betula nana) does layer. A species whose shoots creep stays at 0; one whose shoots end at the ground gets a sourced value; the spruce stays at 0. Stated before code.
- **R3 (proved):** red/green tests that at 0 nothing changes (fn-200's resting test holds) and that at a high value no axis extends along the ground beyond one cycle past contact; the birch's skirt ends at or just above the ground in stills at whole and close views, viewed, beside a weeping-birch reference; `space_hash` changes listed per species (expected only where shoots reached the ground); CPU and device agree; gate and npm test green; the owner's verdict.

## Open design point (plan review, 2026-10-09)

Sag is computed after growth (`grow/settle.rs:39-41,91-98`; the rough lay carries no sag load, `grow/draw.rs:412`), so a strand that reaches the ground only by sagging never shows that contact to the growth draws (`grow/advance.rs:69-86,182-198`). A ground hazard therefore needs contact to reach growth: either the rough lay carries an estimate of sag, or the hazard acts on the final geometry. The host designs this before the spec is planned. Moved off the release map (host, 2026-10-09): the owner likes the ground-running growth, and whether the silver birch should keep it is first a sourcing question.

## Back on the release map (owner, 2026-10-14)

The owner: "we might also want to fix the birch's branches drooping all the way to the ground"; "that's mainly for younger specimen. Older ones will be too high to reach the ground". R1 therefore also measures the birch at 5, 10, 15, 20 and 30 (seeds 1, 7, 42): the height of its lowest living branch (crown base) against the sources' crown lift for open-grown silver birch (a light-demanding species that sheds its shaded lower branches early), and which strands reach the ground at each age. Two causes are possible and are told apart before any design: lower branches the birch should already have shed (crown lift: the shedding and light rules, or the birch's values) and strands that hang long enough to reach the ground from where they should be (the ground rule above). The host designs the fix from R1's numbers; the open design point stays the host's.

## Boundaries

The birch's low branches and the lay's ground rule, and their measurement. A release item (owner, 2026-10-14).
