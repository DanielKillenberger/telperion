## Goal & Context

A tree is a specimen with an age. Age in years is one more continuous input to the tree space, so any family at any age is a valid tree, a walk along the age dimension is the same tree growing, and the same family, seed and age always give the same tree. Growth is live: a running scene advances a specimen by any fraction of a year at whatever rate the game chooses, and the tree grows in place, branches keeping their identity, thickening and extending, with shaded limbs dying and dropping as real open-grown trees self-prune. Every family grows by the same rule; the oak and the spruce are calibrated against open-grown growth curves for height and trunk diameter. Dying, seasons and the environment's influence are later specs. [user]

## Architecture & Data Models

<!-- Rewritten 2026-09-13 at the owner's request as one design rather than a list of dated amendments. - **One specimen, one writer, many readers.** A specimen is a chronicle: the lifetime record of one tree, in which every node, run, placement, leaf cohort and radius keyframe carries the year it was born and, once gone, the year it died. The growth simulation is the chronicle's only writer and it only appends: it stamps births, stamps deaths, and never deletes, moves or overwrites. Everything else is a reader, a pure function of the chronicle and an age: the tree at an age, the change between two ages, the snapshot, the blend a presentation layer draws between two ages, and a step backward. Readers cannot disagree with one another or with the simulation, because there is one record and nothing is recomputed from history. [user]
- **Stepped growth over the existing builder, retained as a frontier.** Production generation is the scaffold queue (`scaffold::generate` consumes whole axes through a queue of growth units), then the radius solve, then `local::append` for local branches and twigs, then shell shedding, then the solve again. Growth over time keeps that order but makes each stage resumable: the specimen retains the scaffold frontier (every axis still growing, its remaining units and its per-axis random stream, already keyed by parent, station and member), the attractor set, and the local frontier (every shoot still able to bud, with its stream). An attractor that is consumed is stamped with the year, never flagged in place. A slice spends a budget of growth units, set by the family's growth rate at the current age, across the scaffold frontier first and the local frontier second, expands the envelope and the attractor reach along the species' curve, decides shedding by vigour, and stamps what it did. A fresh build at age t runs slices from a seedling to t; native builds, wasm builds and a retained advance go through that one implementation, so they agree by construction. The slice runs in Palubicki's order: environment, bud fate, shoots, shedding, width. [user]
- **A yearly slice with the remainder carried.** Monthly slices were the design and the number chose the year: measured 2026-09-13, the mature oak built in 5.906 s at monthly slices and 0.775 s at yearly ones against a half-second target, so the slice is a year and live playback lands one change per tree-year, invisible at any rate above a year a minute. Age is an integer year count plus an integer sub-year remainder at a fixed denominator, never a float, so any sequence of advances that reaches the same age applies exactly the same slices and the result is independent of tick size. A consumer that wants faster growth passes more years per tick; no artificial rate lives in the core, and a long advance is one call, the consumer slicing its own advances if it wants progress. [user]
- **Identities are the chronicle's keys, and storage never compacts.** Every node and run has a generational key, a slot plus a version through a dense slot map, assigned at birth from a per-specimen monotone counter and never reused; a node's parent and run are identities, never indices. A dead node keeps its slot with its death stamp, so nothing is ever renumbered and no index-to-identity map has to be rebuilt; the structural-before-local order the surface builder needs is a property of the packed read, produced when a consumer reads, not of storage. A stale key fails loudly instead of resolving to a reused slot. [paraphrase]
- **Thickening as keyframes.** Structural radii come from the pipe-model solve, and the trunk radius it distributes is a fraction of the envelope's height, so the trunk and every structural branch thicken as the envelope grows; local branches and twigs are re-derived from their parent's current radius by the existing child-radius rule, so a surviving local branch never thins as its parent thickens. The solve runs every simulated year, over the paths from that year's changed nodes only, whether the year was reached by one long advance or many short ones, so the keyframes it writes are canonical: a node records a radius keyframe, the year and its radii, only when it thickened by more than the family's resize tolerance, and the radius at any age is the last keyframe at or before it, monotone non-decreasing across extension, forks and shedding because the keyframes are the memory. What runs once per advance is the output work only: placement re-derivation and the packed read. An advance from year 0 to year 10 therefore leaves exactly the keyframes ten yearly advances leave. [paraphrase]
- **Shoot state on nodes.** Each node carries its birth year, its bud fate as terminal or lateral, and a vigour proxy read from its depth inside the crown at the current age. Vigour drives extension and survival; dominance and the light-response benchmark stay with fn-21, which extends this state rather than replacing it. The growth traits take the vocabulary of the self-organizing tree literature and its maintained implementations, growth rate, apical control and its loss with age, bud extinction, thickness accumulation and age factors, pruning by age and order, leaf hang time, mapped onto the existing habit traits where one already exists. [user]
- **Shedding by vigour, as a death stamp.** A shoot whose vigour stays below its family's threshold for the family's tolerance period dies with its subtree; the existing shell shedding is one input to vigour rather than a separate pass. The decision is made from state snapshotted at slice start, with a fixed comparison side at thresholds and a cap on sheds per slice, and its effect is a death stamp on every node, run and placement of the subtree. Nothing is removed and no survivor moves; a shed run leaves the live structure by ceasing to be alive at that year. [user]
- **Growth traits, not a maturity age.** A family carries numeric growth traits, the rate and shape by which height and girth approach the authored envelope, a leaf lifetime in years, and the resize tolerance; maturity is what those traits produce, never a field. Today's preset parameters describe the full-grown envelope, and the age at which a preset reaches its current look is derived and documented, not authored. A point between two families blends their growth traits and is a valid tree at any age. [user]
- **Foliage by living shoot and cohort.** Ratified 2026-09-13 after the earlier rule, leaves from birth for the lifetime only, left 13 placements on the mature oak and 120 on the mature spruce. A living leaf-bearing shoot flushes a cohort of leaves every year it lives and each cohort persists for the family's leaf lifetime, so a mature twig keeps its foliage for as long as it lives; older wood goes bare because it thickened past the bearing diameter, which is the twig anatomy's existing rule. Nothing is stamped for a cohort, because every flush is the same flush: a station on a shoot belongs to a cohort offset spread over the lifetime, its leaf is present at an age when the shoot is alive and below the bearing diameter and the shoot's age is at least that offset, and it keeps one placement identity for the life of the shoot, a replaced leaf being the same leaf without seasons. A shoot therefore fills in over its first lifetime years, from birth for the oak and over several years for the spruce's needles, and then holds; foliage at maturity is a function of the living shoots, nothing is stamped after saturation, and generation work does not grow with the world's age. Structural renewal at maturity, new twigs replacing shed ones, is a later rule beside dying and seasons. Placements carry the identity of the shoot and station they sit on; a station's transform is derived from the wood's positions, radii, frames and surface contact, so a surviving placement moves when any wood its transform reads changed: its own run, and the neighbouring runs its surface contact touches, the same dependency set the contact cache keys on. [user]
- **A slice costs what grew, not what exists.** A slice does structural growth and the shedding decision only; shedding reads crown depth, which needs no radii, so the radius solve runs per year over changed paths only, the placement re-derivation runs once at the end of an advance over runs whose wood changed, and neither touches the whole tree. Structural births write into headroom and move no local storage; a shoot that must wait sleeps until the year the crown can reach it rather than being retried every slice; and with no compaction there is no remap. A build to age t costs about a fresh build today plus a per-slice term that is bookkeeping; a whole-tree pass per slice is the failure mode R10's measurement exists to catch. [user]
- **Growth saturates, so age past maturity is free.** The unit budget a slice receives follows the species' sigmoidal curve toward zero as the tree nears its full-grown envelope; a slice whose budget rounds to zero changes nothing and is jumped over from the curve, and with no new growth crown depth, vigour and foliage are unchanged so nothing sheds and nothing is stamped. A build to any age beyond maturity therefore costs the build to maturity, and a consumer's load time is bounded by the species, not by the age of its world. [user]
- **Any age at or before the frontier is a filter.** The frontier is the age the simulation has reached. The tree at an age at or before it is the set of elements born at or before that age and not dead by it, with radii from the last keyframe at or before it and foliage from the cohorts inside the lifetime window; it costs a pass over the chronicle and no simulation, and it is byte-identical to a fresh build to that age. The change record between two ages is derived from the stamps in the interval: runs and placements born or dead, runs with a keyframe, stations whose cohort offset was reached, and every placement whose transform reads a run that gained a keyframe or died in the interval, its own or a neighbour it touches, so sibling thickening and sibling removal both move the leaves they move; no diff of whole states is computed and `resized` needs no separate tolerance because the keyframes already carry it. A step backward is the same filter one year earlier; a step forward past the frontier is one slice of simulation. [user]
- **The chronicle is bounded by a cap, not by the world's age.** A specimen carries a history cap in years, a numeric setting of the consumer with a large default: elements dead for longer than the cap below the frontier are compacted away, so a consumer that never scrubs pays nothing for the past, one that scrubs a century keeps a century, and a scrub past the cap is a rebuild from seed. The cap changes what a specimen remembers, never what it grows: with any cap, the tree at the frontier is the same bytes. [paraphrase]
- **Growth is lazy and persistence is the seed.** Because building at an age and advancing to it agree byte for byte, a consumer stores nothing for a tree that only grew: family, seed and the world clock regenerate it, only specimens in view are advanced each tick, and any specimen catches up on load by a build to the clock's age. What a consumer stores is divergence, an event log of identity, event and time replayed onto the regenerated tree, which fn-16 defines. The specimen snapshot is the chronicle itself with its frontier, in the shape of the field's existing schema-1 portable snapshot, the optional exception for a tree that must load without a build and never the default; it never carries the mesh. [user]
- **The skeleton is the persistent incremental state.** Identities and stamps are append-only across a specimen's life so that a later spec can sweep the wood surface on the GPU from the chronicle alone, at any age, and a presentation layer can blend between two ages from the stamps inside the interval; in this spec the wood surface is rebuilt and re-submitted per advance while leaf placements update from the change record. [user]

## API Contracts

- **Age is a family field**, in years, validated like every field and walked by the blend; a preset states the age its calibration was judged at only as documentation. [user]
- **A retained specimen**, native and in the wasm binding as a handle in the shape of the field handle, built from a family and its age, with a history cap, and advanced by a number of years; advancing returns the change record over identities for the interval it covered. The handle reads at its frontier by default and at any age at or before the frontier on request, returning the same outputs a fresh build to that age returns; the change record between any two ages at or before the frontier is available on request. Applying a record to the consumer's previous buffers reproduces exactly the buffers a fresh read at the later age returns. [user]
- **A specimen snapshot**, the chronicle with its frontier and retained frontiers, in the shape of the field's schema-1 portable snapshot, proportional to the chronicle, exported from and imported into a specimen. A snapshot imported and advanced agrees byte for byte with a fresh build to the same age, and a snapshot read at any age at or before its frontier agrees with a fresh build to that age; the mesh is never part of it. [user]
- **Errors** are explicit and leave the specimen usable: a negative advance is refused naming the value; an age outside its range is refused naming the field; a read at an age beyond the frontier is refused naming the frontier; a read at an age older than the cap allows is refused naming the cap; a node ceiling reached mid-growth stops growth with the existing capped diagnostic and the specimen at the last complete slice, and the next advance refuses with the same diagnostic until the ceiling is raised. A handle whose specimen was released or rebuilt is invalid, as the field handle is today. [paraphrase]
- **The harness** gains an age dial that scrubs at or below the frontier by reading and beyond it by advancing, a rebuild at the chosen age, and a play control that advances live at a years-per-second rate chosen on the page; all drive the one specimen. [user]

## Edge Cases & Constraints

- **Any family grows.** The rule reads only numeric traits; no species branch, no preset name, in the growth code. The Two Trees take the same age and must build and grow at every age, with no reference or verdict applied to them here. [user]
- **Determinism across tick sizes** is the load-bearing property: one jump and a chain of small advances to the same age must be byte-identical, including which limbs shed and in which slice. Random draws are keyed by identity and slice, never by call order; the scaffold's per-axis streams already are. Age accumulates as an integer year count with an integer sub-year remainder in a fixed denominator, never a float. The frontier is iterated and every reduction summed in identity order, never storage order. Shedding is decided from state snapshotted at slice start with a fixed comparison side at thresholds and a cap on sheds per slice. Every transcendental in the growth path goes through one pinned pure-Rust implementation, since the standard library's sine, cosine, exponent and power differ between native and wasm; a native-to-wasm parity probe over today's builder runs before growth is built. [user]
- **Growth after twigs.** New structural growth lands after local branches and twigs exist; storage never reorders and the packed read presents the structural segment before the local one, identities unchanged. Shedding with surviving siblings stamps the shed subtree and touches no survivor. Both are pinned by tests on identities and on output reconciliation. [paraphrase]
- **Backward is a read, within the cap.** Scrubbing to an earlier age at or below the frontier is a filter over the chronicle, byte-identical to a fresh build to that age and costing no simulation; scrubbing further back than the history cap keeps is a rebuild from seed at about the cost of today's build. No history need be persisted: the chronicle is regenerated from family, seed and age. [user]
- **Cost is measured, not yet gated.** The cost of one monthly slice and of a full mature build on the oak and the spruce is recorded beside today's build time in the report; the per-slice fixed cost, chiefly the radius update and the placement re-derivation, is the number a fast-forwarding game pays twenty times a second at a century a minute. [user]
- **Today's presets may drift** within a judged tolerance when the growth rule needs it; identity pins are re-pinned with the reason recorded each time the rule requires it, the parity fix and the monthly build path being two such reasons, and the mature oak and spruce are judged again by the owner at R11. Age and the growth traits reach the JSON wire the way fn-14's material row did: the frozen fn-19 protocol test drops the fields the evidence predates before comparing, since that evidence states no age. [user]
- **Open-grown references first.** The calibration sources open-grown height and diameter curves for the oak and the spruce the way fn-9 sourced its profiles, with sources and checksums in the report; stand-grown yield tables are the fallback, named as such. Where the curve and the specimen's look disagree, the deviation is recorded and the owner judges which rules at that age. [user]
- **Coupling named.** fn-4 and fn-20 edit the wood surface and terminal radii; fn-21 extends the shoot state; fn-16 adds events on top of this timeline; fn-27's caster prefix re-sorts runs by radius at submit and continues to do so per advance. [inferred]

## Approach, in one pass

One task builds the whole spec in the order below; the owner's standing rule is one task per spec, so the order and the tests live here rather than in a task breakdown. Each numbered step lands with its tests green before the next begins.

1. **Identities and the retained frontier.** Add the identity counter and per-node identity, the index-to-identity map, and the retained scaffold, attractor and local frontiers as a `Specimen` beside `Tree`; make the existing whole build a specimen grown to the mature age in one call. Tests: identities survive growth after twigs and shedding with surviving siblings; the crossover invariant holds after every slice; the byte-identity of a specimen grown to the mature age against itself across two runs.
2. **The yearly slice and replay equivalence** (built monthly, chosen yearly by the number; see the slice bullet). The unit budget from the growth traits, the envelope and attractor expansion along the curve shape, the slice loop with the remainder carried. Tests: any partition of an advance into slices gives the same tree; a fresh build at t equals advance-to-t, byte for byte; a negative advance and an out-of-range age refuse naming the value; the node ceiling mid-growth leaves the last complete slice with the capped diagnostic and the next advance refuses.
3. **Thickening and shedding.** The per-slice radius update for structural and local wood; vigour from crown depth and birth year; shedding by threshold and tolerance with the shell rule folded in. Tests: surviving radii monotone non-decreasing across extension, forks and shedding; a shoot under the threshold for the period is shed in the expected slice and never earlier.
4. **The chronicle.** Death stamps in place of removal and compaction, with the packed read filtering dead elements and ordering the structural segment first; attractor consumption and vigour counters as stamped events; radius keyframes under the resize tolerance; leaf cohorts as stamped events under the ratified foliage rule; the read at any age at or below the frontier; the change record as the stamps in an interval; the history cap. Tests: a specimen grown and shed, read at several ages, equals a fresh build at each, byte for byte, including one advanced straight from seed to age ten against ten yearly advances, every intermediate read and change record compared; a record applied to the earlier read equals the later read, including a surface-contact case; no dead element appears in any read; the mature oak and spruce keep their foliage at maturity plus twice the lifetime (R12); the cap compacts only what it says and leaves the frontier's bytes unchanged.
5. **Bindings and the page.** The wasm specimen handle with build, advance, read-at-age and change-between-ages, invalid after release or rebuild; the native API mirrored; the snapshot as the chronicle with its frontier, imported and advanced or read agreeing byte for byte with fresh builds; the harness age dial reading within the frontier and advancing beyond it, the rebuild, and the play rate, all driving the one specimen; the wood re-submitted and placements updated from the record. Tests: handle recovery after a failed rebuild, the existing binding ownership tests extended, the snapshot round trip, and native-to-wasm parity for every preset.
6. **Moved to fn-30:** curves and calibration, then routing and the re-pin.
7. **Cost and the report.** Measure the per-slice cost, the mature build, the snapshot round trip, a read at an age and a change record on both trees with the core's timers; write the cost report in fn-14's shape from those numbers and the commit record. The age strips, the mature stills and the R11 verdict are fn-30's.

## Boundaries

Environmental competition, damage response and seasonal appearance are separate extensions; the first pass establishes age-driven development. [inferred]

- No death of the whole tree; growth to maturity and beyond by the same rule, dying is a later spec and optional for games. [user]
- No seasons, no leaf flush or fall beyond the cohort rule: a living twig flushes every year and a cohort persists for the lifetime, nothing more. [user]
- No environment or damage response; fn-16 adds events on this timeline. [user]
- No foliage shape variation and no light-response benchmark; fn-21 keeps them on top of this spec's shoot state. [user]
- No forest-scale growth and no GPU-swept wood; the skeleton is shaped so a later spec can sweep the wood on the GPU. [user]
- No artificial growth rate in the core; a consumer chooses years per tick. [user]

## Decision Context

### Motivation

Growth is the strategy's lifecycle promise made real: one persistent botanical structure from trunk to twig that a game can place at any age and grow live, generated from a seed rather than swapped between baked stages. Live, incremental growth was chosen over a static age dial because a running scene is the use; determinism without history was chosen so a game never has to persist a tree's past; identity with natural shedding was chosen over strict identity because real open-grown trees self-prune. The owner values realism first, so calibration targets open-grown curves and the owner judges the residual. [user]

### Implementation Tradeoffs

- Stepped growth over a closed-form evaluation at any age: the builder already steps, the skeleton stage is a small fraction of a build (11 ms of 60 for the ordinary tree, 315 of 1,608 ms for the giant, mesh-free, 2026-09-06), advancing is the same loop continued, and equivalence holds by construction; feedback effects for fn-16 attach naturally. The closed-form alternative was examined on 2026-09-13 with the implementer's review: emitting N elements costs O(N) however the tree is evaluated, so a closed form would make cost independent of elapsed steps rather than small, and it would need an explicit decision about which history-dependent behaviours, crown competition, vigour-driven shedding and the cohorts, are kept as functions of age. The chronicle keeps them all and gives the scrubbing the closed form promised. [user]

### docs-scout
- **Palubicki et al. 2009, self-organizing tree models** — one iteration is one season in the order estimate environment, determine bud fate, append shoots, shed branches, update width; the spec's slice order is this loop and should say so. Source: https://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/selforg.sig2009.html
- **Vigour and apical control, extended Borchert–Honda** — resource splits between a terminal and its laterals by one trait λ with λ above 0.5 excurrent (spruce) and below decurrent (oak); the spec's unit budget across the frontier is that split and the habit traits already carry the oak and spruce forms, so no new species branch is needed, and the paper's own space-colonization variant with binary light is the spec's crown-depth proxy. Source: same paper
- **Shedding, Takenaka 1994 via Měch and Prusinkiewicz 1996** — shed when light gathered over branch size falls under a threshold; Palubicki warns the binary-light variant sheds a branch the moment it stops growing, so the spec's tolerance period is what makes shedding by a proxy usable. Source: same paper, section on branch shedding
- **Thickening** — the pipe model as the n-th power sum of child widths with n between 2 and 3, and width is never decreased when branches are shed, so the model keeps a memory of past branches; independent support for the spec's monotone radius rule and its identity-retained frontier. Source: Macdonald 1983 via Palubicki 2009; Shinozaki et al. 1964, review https://academic.oup.com/aob/article/121/5/773/4820933
- **Plastic Trees, Pirk et al. 2012** — lazy local re-transform of a skeleton only where the environment changed, to avoid re-running the growth procedure; the closest prior art to the retained frontier plus change record. Source: https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/bitstream/123456789/21500/2/Pirk_215002.pdf
- **Functional-structural plant models (LIGNUM, GreenLab, AMAPsim)** step annually and allocate a carbon budget; the spec's unit budget from growth traits is a geometric surrogate with no photosynthesis, a simplification to record, not a novelty. Source: https://sal.aalto.fi/publications/pdf-files/rper09.pdf, https://arxiv.org/pdf/q-bio/0701026
- **Weber and Penn 1995** has no time dimension; the alternative the spec is not using. Source: the paper, and openalea/weberpenn below
- **Quercus garryana, Silvics of North America (Stein 1990)** — open-grown form described; mature 15 to 27 m at 60 to 100 cm DBH; 6 to 8 rings per cm common; the forest-grown pair 95 to 135 years to 24 m and 48 cm is stand-grown; no age curve. Source: https://research.fs.usda.gov/silvics/oregon-white-oak
- **Gould, Harrington and Devine 2011, Growth of Oregon white oak** — diameter increment against size and competition with an explicit open-grown class, and DBH to height; age is not a predictor. Source: https://cascadiaprairieoak.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/Gould-P.J.-C.A.-Harrington-and-W.D.-Devine.-2011.-Growth-of-Oregon-White-Oak-Quercus-garryana.pdf
- **Saltspring Island dendrochronology** — 39 savanna-grown Garry oaks crossdated 1825 to 2005; ring widths give cumulative radius against age; no heights. The closest real DBH-age series for the oak. Source: https://bioone.org/journals/tree-ring-research/volume-68/issue-1/2011-5.1/Dendrochronological-Potential-of-Quercus-garryana-Saltspring-Island-British-Columbia/10.3959/2011-5.1.short
- **GOERT** publishes stewardship material, no growth or age tables; the spec's assumption of GOERT growth data is unconfirmed. Source: https://goert.ca/species/quercus-garryana/
- **Hasenauer 1997, open-grown trees in Austria** — 435 genuinely open-grown trees of 11 species including Picea abies: crown width, DBH to height, crown base, taper; no age. Best open-grown spruce shape source. Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378112797000571
- **Pretzsch et al. 2015, crown size in urban centres, parks and forests** — species-specific open-grown crown-radius to stem-diameter allometry for 22 species; verify Picea abies and an oak are among them; diameter-indexed, not age-indexed. Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1618866715000473
- **Pretzsch and Caicoya 2025, estimating tree age from height** — a sigmoidal height-age function parameterized for Picea abies and Quercus robur/petraea, fitted on dominant trees of long-term plots, so stand-grown; the best height-age fit for the spruce and a proxy for the oak; coefficients unverified. Source: https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a446fa8a4696498498826f7f47185469b6140222
- **Bavarian yield-table extracts (Assmann and Franz 1963 spruce, Jüttner 1955 oak)** — stand-grown, age in ten-year steps with heights and mean diameter per site class; a single checksummable PDF, the practical R8 fallback for the age axis. Source: https://www.forstpraxis.de/sites/forstpraxis.de/files/2023-07/AFZ_FHJ_Kalender_2024_306_318_Ertragstafeln_ste_OK.pdf
- **Needle turnover of Norway spruce** — roughly six to seven needle cohorts, about half a cohort shed by 5.5 years and all by about 12; supports a spruce leaf lifetime near five to six years against the oak's one; figures from a search snippet, verify in the paper. Source: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233391503_Needle_and_branch_biomass_turnover_rates_of_Norway_spruce_Picea_abies
- **USDA Urban Tree Database (McPherson, van Doorn and Peper 2016)** — the only source found that fits age to DBH directly, plus DBH to height and crown, over about 14,000 street and park trees with age recorded; differencing gives per-year increments; whether Quercus garryana and Picea abies are among its species could not be verified through the fetch block and must be checked first. Source: https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/52933, data https://www.fs.usda.gov/rds/archive/catalog/RDS-2016-0005

Source: the scout's synthesis over the items above

Source: https://blogg.slu.se/forest-biometrics/2017/03/11/the-chapman-richards-growth-function/

### practice-scout
- **Gotcha:** only add, subtract, multiply, divide and square root are bit-identical across targets in Rust; sin, cos, exp and powf vary by platform and version and wasm is named, so every transcendental in the growth path must go through one pinned pure-Rust implementation such as the libm crate or R5's byte-identity fails between native and wasm. Source: https://github.com/rust-lang/rust/issues/150323, https://rust-lang.github.io/rfcs/3514-float-semantics.html
- **Gotcha:** wasm arithmetic is IEEE deterministic, so parity holds if no fused multiply-add or reassociation differs between builds; keep the simulation exact and the renderer free. Source: https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/determinism/
- **Gotcha:** the budget split across the frontier must iterate in identity order, never storage order, or compaction permutes the random draws. Source: scout synthesis
- **Gotcha:** accumulate age as an integer month counter plus an integer sub-month remainder in a fixed denominator, never a float accumulator, or partitions of an advance drift apart. Source: https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/
- **Gotcha:** decide shedding from state snapshotted at slice start, with a fixed comparison side at thresholds and a fixed traversal order for every reduction, or which limb sheds depends on visit order. Source: scout synthesis
- **Gotcha:** compact-and-renumber with raw index handles is the ABA bug; generational keys (slot plus version) make stale handles fail loudly, and a dense slot map is the shape the spec wants rather than a hand-rolled index map. Source: https://docs.rs/slotmap/latest/slotmap/
- **Gotcha:** derive the change record from a diff of identity sets, not from appends at mutation sites, and give resized a tolerance or every run reports resized every slice. Source: scout synthesis
- **Gotcha:** in validated self-organizing models the sensitive parameters are resource flow and shedding rate; failure modes are leader death under basitony and progressive lower-crown thinning from high shedding; apical control that weakens with age is what turns young-excurrent into old-decurrent and is a growth trait, not a constant. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11128038/, Palubicki 2009
- **Gotcha:** oscillating vigour comes from same-slice feedback; the tolerance period is the hysteresis, and cap sheds per slice. Source: scout synthesis
- **Gotcha:** a spatial index for attractor association is the per-slice cost lever in space colonization, and the per-slice fixed costs scale with live node count, about 1,200 solves for a century at monthly slices. Source: https://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/colonization.egwnp2007.large.pdf
- **Gotcha:** Chapman-Richards asymptote and origin are ill-conditioned extrapolations; reparameterize to expected values at known ages, which R8's three ages already are. Source: http://derekogle.com/fishR/examples/oldFishRVignettes/VonBertalanffy.pdf
- **Gotcha:** open versus stand-grown is a diameter difference, not a height one; a stand-grown table under-reports DBH, so state the height-to-diameter ratio per age. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2987550/
- **Gotcha:** urban trees are not truly open-grown (pruning, soil, irrigation); name that caveat when using the Urban Tree Database. Source: https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/41665.pdf
- **Measure first:** the radius solve alone per slice on today's mature oak; a native-to-wasm bit-parity probe over the existing builder by hashing the node buffer; the sourced curve against today's mature presets before fitting; the crown-depth vigour distribution on today's mature tree to see whether one threshold separates shed from keep at every age. Source: scout synthesis
