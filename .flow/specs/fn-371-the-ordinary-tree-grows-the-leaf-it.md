# The ordinary tree grows the leaf it draws

Child of fn-351 (Phase 2's landing: no tree worse than at the last landing). Host-written, 14 Nov 2026. Builds on `fn-351-candidate`.

## Problem

On the candidate the ordinary tree at its preset age is worse than the base's (`base-2026-11-11`), seen by the host on seeds 1001 and 1007 (`fn-364-…/sheets/ordinary.jpg`): a 13.2 and 12.6 m tree on a trunk of 9 and 10 cm where the base stands on 22 and 25 cm, with 25 and 32 m² of drawn leaf against 35 and 46. Height over trunk diameter is about 140; no tree stands so. Since fn-354 the trunk is what the tree's leaf earns through its sapwood row, and the ordinary tree grows little leaf: fn-370's reading (`fn-370-…/R1.md`) measures 18.4 and 23.2 m² in the grower, and shows why: its drawn blade is 44.4 cm² (the element's 42.51 cm² at size 1 with its size variation) where the grower's `leaf_area` says 15.7 cm², the beech's measured leaf. The ordinary tree is no species; every one of its rows is a listed estimate, and here two estimates of the same leaf disagree by 2.83 times. The tree is drawn with one leaf and grown, shaded and girthed with another.

## The design (host)

The ordinary tree's leaf is the leaf it draws. Its `leafArea` rows take the area of its own drawn blade (the element's area at its `/canopy/size`, with the mean of its size variation, as `leaflet.rs` draws it: the number fn-370's probe reports, to be recomputed from the rows, not typed from that report), wherever they state the beech's 15.7 cm² today, with a comment saying it is the drawn blade's area and a listed estimate. No other row changes; no other preset changes. This is not a tuning of the trunk: the leaf is set by what is drawn, and the trunk follows by the sapwood rule already in place.

Expected on paper: the grower's leaf about 2.8 times before the light answers (deeper self-shade takes some back), so about 45 to 60 m²; sections with the leaf, so the trunk about 1.5 to 1.7 times, 14 to 17 cm; the drawn leaf moves little, since the plan counts stations by length (up or down by under a quarter through the changed tree). The builder returns if the trunk at 40 ends under 13 cm or over 22 cm, if the height moves by more than a fifth, or if the drawn tree reads barer than today's.

## Acceptance Criteria

- **R1.** The reading first: every `leafArea` row of the ordinary tree and where each value came from (its comment, git blame), the drawn blade's area computed from its element and canopy rows with the arithmetic shown, the ordinary tree's sapwood row, and what else reads the ordinary tree's values (the walks' base tree, blends, tests that pin it).
- **R2.** The rows changed as designed; the preset files regenerated by their generators.
- **R3.** Measured before and after on seeds 1, 7, 1001, 1007 at 40 and 80: height, spread, trunk at 1.3 m, the grower's leaf, the drawn leaf, phytomers, seconds; height over trunk beside it.
- **R4.** Pins and bounds that move, each by its own procedure and listed with its tree; no other preset's digest moves (the pin, by name). A walk or blend bound is never loosened: return with the numbers if one fails for a reason other than the ordinary tree having changed.
- **R5.** One sheet: base (`base-2026-11-11`), the candidate, this branch, the ordinary tree whole and mid on seeds 1001 and 1007 at 40, with height, trunk and leaf under each tile, for the host's verdict.
- **R6.** Narrow tests while building; the two sub-second guards before finishing.

## Stops

- The numbers outside the expected band (see the design).
- The ordinary tree being the base of tests whose trees would all move (the walks): list them with their count and return before repinning more than ten pins.

## Boundaries

The ordinary tree's `leafArea` rows and what pins them. No rule, no other row, no other preset.

## Rigor

Opus builds; heavy runs through `telperion-heavy` with `TELPERION_HEAVY_RESERVE=8`, asking for the memory a job needs, GPU stills with `--gpu 2` one at a time, in the background, checked by the clock. The host views the sheet and gives the verdict.
