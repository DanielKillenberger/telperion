- Cavity derivation (wood.wgsl bark_light, round 5): bark_colour_range() in bark.wgsl returns the field's face mean h0 (ridge mean plus the integrated plate and flake bands) and its real amplitude s (peak depth times BARK_DEPTH_RANGE.y plus the face bands), both from the field's own constants times ridgeScale * maturity * girth; t = clamp((height - h0) / s, -1, 1), crest = max(t,0) * maturity, fissure = max(-t,0) * maturity, so the flat face takes the base colour with no cavity and no tint and the constant-height far path converges to the face colour; albedo A = B*(1 - k*fissure) + F*fissure + C*crest, piecewise affine with one kink at the face mean. Round 4's map (crest = clamp(height / (0.35*ridgeScale), 0, maturity), fissure = maturity - crest) called 60-100% of the face a fissure because the field rarely exceeds 0.15 ridgeScale, which darkened the whole trunk and tinted it blue under sky light; at the trunk camera the resolved oak face is now 71.24% face, 22.59% fissure, 6.17% crest by blend weight. Before round 5 the map was a single affine map (a multiplicative cavity on tinted colour was quadratic in height and broke the 4x distance mean). Both sun and sky multiply A; sheen takes the same 1 - k*f. An independent geometric contact term, max(ground contact within one radius, clamp(-radius * minimum principal curvature, 0, 1) from the existing normal derivatives), multiplies the sum: that is the answer to the parked socket question, joined necks darken, disconnected crossing limbs do not (fn-4/fn-20).
- Sky occlusion: occluded_ambient(n, depth) in common.wgsl removes only the sky hemisphere by crown depth (the placement ellipsoid the generator already knows), on wood by fragment depth and on leaves by placement depth; ground bounce stays; fn-14's interior darkening still multiplies after it. No new light, no shadow-map change, no vertex layout change; the wood hash is identical with rows on and off (colour_cavity.rs).
