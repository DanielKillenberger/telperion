## Identity

Oregon white oak is one of only four deciduous oaks native to the West Coast, and a member of the beech family. [OWIC-OAK](sources/OWIC-OAK.md)
It is native to western British Columbia, Washington, Oregon and northwestern California. [OSU-OAK](sources/OSU-OAK.md)
The massive branching trunks and broad crowns of old white oaks are a characteristic feature of valley woodlands in the Pacific Northwest. [OWIC-OAK](sources/OWIC-OAK.md)
It is a persistent climax or sub-climax species on dry sites and under periodic fire, and an early successional one on better sites, where Douglas-fir and bigleaf maple replace it once fire is excluded. [OWIC-OAK](sources/OWIC-OAK.md)
It wants sun and a dry soil in summer, roots on a deep taproot, and takes root rot from summer watering. [OSU-OAK](sources/OSU-OAK.md)

## Size and growth

Under favourable conditions a mature tree reaches the height and breast-height diameter the table below records, and the maxima on record stand well above that range. [USFS-OAK](sources/USFS-OAK.md)
Growth is generally slow in both height and diameter, and stump sprouts are the one fast exception, in their first years only. [OWIC-OAK](sources/OWIC-OAK.md)
In closed forest stands on good sites the same species makes narrow crowns on straight, clear stems, so the open-grown form this record describes is one of two the tree can take. [OWIC-OAK](sources/OWIC-OAK.md)
Forest-grown trees of that second form carry slim straight boles and fine side branches; open-grown ones occupy much more space for much less usable volume. [USFS-OAK](sources/USFS-OAK.md)

## Crown and habit

An open-grown tree develops a very broad, rounded crown whose width may equal the tree's total height, over a short and massive bole. [OWIC-OAK](sources/OWIC-OAK.md)
The limbs that fill it are massive and crooked, heavy and ascending, and the outline they make is rugged and irregular rather than a smooth shell. [OSU-OAK](sources/OSU-OAK.md) [OWIC-OAK](sources/OWIC-OAK.md)
Branch development on an open-grown tree may be very dense, while in a closed stand the side branches stay sparse. [USFS-OAK](sources/USFS-OAK.md)
Young trees are often shrubby, and on poor sites and in open stands the boles stay short and crooked. [OSU-OAK](sources/OSU-OAK.md) [OWIC-OAK](sources/OWIC-OAK.md)
Below ground the form is a deep taproot with a well-developed lateral system, which leaves the tree very windfirm even in wet ground. [USFS-OAK](sources/USFS-OAK.md)

## Bark

The bark is greyish, and may be shaggy or carry shallow ridges and fissures. [OSU-OAK-ID](sources/OSU-OAK-ID.md)
On a large tree it is thick enough to resist fire, while smaller trees are generally killed or badly injured by it. [OWIC-OAK](sources/OWIC-OAK.md)

## Leaves

The leaves are simple, alternate and deciduous, each blade carried on a visible petiole rather than clustered at the twig. [OSU-OAK](sources/OSU-OAK.md) [OSU-OAK-ID](sources/OSU-OAK-ID.md)
The blade is pinnately lobed, with rounded and often irregular lobes, and it carries none of the bristle tips that mark the black oaks. [OSU-OAK-ID](sources/OSU-OAK-ID.md)
The two OSU descriptions differ on how many lobes a blade carries, so the count reads as a range rather than an identity mark. [OSU-OAK](sources/OSU-OAK.md) [OSU-OAK-ID](sources/OSU-OAK-ID.md)
Autumn colour is a saddle brown, occasionally tinted gold or dull red. [OSU-OAK](sources/OSU-OAK.md)
