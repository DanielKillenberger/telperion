_**Phoenix dactylifera**_, commonly known as the **date palm**, is a flowering-plant species in the palm family [Arecaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arecaceae "Arecaceae"), native to the region from the [Gulf States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_states_of_the_Persian_Gulf "Arab states of the Persian Gulf") and Iraq to Pakistan and India.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-kew-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-chih-2)[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-3) It is cultivated for its edible sweet [fruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#Fruit) called **dates**. The species is widely cultivated across [northern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africa "North Africa"), the Middle East, Australia, [South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia") and [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia"), the coastal [Mediterranean basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_basin "Mediterranean basin"), and [Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_California "Southern California").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-kew-1) It is [naturalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalisation_(biology) "Naturalisation (biology)") in many [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropics "Tropics") and [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropics "Subtropics") regions worldwide.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-kew-1) _P.dactylifera_ is the [type species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_species "Type species") of genus _[Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_(plant) "Phoenix (plant)")_, which contains 12–19 species of [wild date palms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_date_palm "Wild date palm").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Krueger-4)

Date palms reach up to 30 metres (98ft) in height,[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-chih-2) growing singly or in [clumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clumping_(biology) "Clumping (biology)") with several stems from a single root system. Slow-growing, they can reach over 100years of age when maintained properly.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Hodel-5) Their fruits are oval-cylindrical, 3 to 7 centimetres (1 to 3 inches) long, and about 2.5cm (1in) in diameter, with colour ranging from dark brown to bright red or yellow, depending on variety. Containing 63–64% sugar by mass when dried, dates are consumed as sweet snacks on their own or with [confections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confectionery "Confectionery").

There is [archaeological evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_evidence "Archaeological evidence") of date cultivation in Arabia since the 6thmillennium BCE.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-judea-6) Dates are "emblematic of [oasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oasis "Oasis") agriculture and highly symbolic in Christian and Jewish religions".[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-judea-6)

## Description

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Description")\]

Date palms reach up to 30 metres (100 feet) in height, growing singly or forming a clump with several stems from a single root system.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Hodel-5) Date palms grow from average temperature range of 12.7 to 27.5°C and are slow-growing.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-chih-2) They can reach over 100 years of age when maintained properly.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Hodel-5) The roots have [pneumatodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumatode "Pneumatode").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Halli-Mangenot-1986-7) The [leaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaves "Leaves") are 4–6m (13–20ft) long, with spines on the [petiole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petiole_(botany) "Petiole (botany)"), and [pinnate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinnate "Pinnate"), with about 150 leaflets. The leaflets are 30 centimetres (12 inches) long and 2cm (1in) wide. The full span of the crown ranges from 6–10m (20–33ft).

The date palm is [dioecious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioecious "Dioecious"), having separate male and female plants. Like all members of genus Phoenix, sex determination occurs through an [XY sex-determination system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XY_sex-determination_system "XY sex-determination system"), where three genes that are conserved in all males and absent in all females have been identified.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-8) They can be easily grown from seed, but only 50% of seedlings will be female and hence fruit-bearing, and dates from seedling plants are often smaller and of poorer quality. Most commercial plantations thus use [cuttings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting_(plant) "Cutting (plant)") of heavily cropping cultivars. Plants grown from cuttings will fruit 2–3 years earlier than seedling plants.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f2/%D7%93%D7%A7%D7%9C_%D7%AA%D7%9E%D7%A8_%D7%9E%D7%A6%D7%95%D7%99_%D7%97%D7%AA%D7%9A_%D7%92%D7%96%D7%A2_%D7%93%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%99_%D7%A2%D7%A5_%283%29.jpg/250px-%D7%93%D7%A7%D7%9C_%D7%AA%D7%9E%D7%A8_%D7%9E%D7%A6%D7%95%D7%99_%D7%97%D7%AA%D7%9A_%D7%92%D7%96%D7%A2_%D7%93%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%99_%D7%A2%D7%A5_%283%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:%D7%93%D7%A7%D7%9C_%D7%AA%D7%9E%D7%A8_%D7%9E%D7%A6%D7%95%D7%99_%D7%97%D7%AA%D7%9A_%D7%92%D7%96%D7%A2_%D7%93%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%99_%D7%A2%D7%A5_(3).jpg) _Phoenix dactylifera_ trunk section. As with other members of the [palm family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arecaceae "Arecaceae"), date palms do not produce annual [tree rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_rings "Tree rings").[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Phoenix_dactylifera_0zz.jpg/250px-Phoenix_dactylifera_0zz.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Phoenix_dactylifera_0zz.jpg) Leaves of the date palm

Dates are naturally wind- [pollinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollination "Pollination"), but in traditional oasis horticulture and modern commercial orchards, they are entirely [hand-pollinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand-pollination "Hand-pollination"). Natural pollination occurs with about an equal number of male and female plants. With assistance, one male can pollinate up to 100 females. Since the males are of value only as pollinators, they are usually pruned in favor of fruit-producing female plants. Some growers do not maintain male plants, as male flowers become available at local markets at pollination time. Manual pollination is done by skilled labourers on ladders, or by use of a wind machine. In some areas, such as [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq "Iraq"), the pollinator climbs the tree using a special climbing tool that wraps around the tree trunk and the climber's back (called تبلية in Arabic) to keep him attached to the trunk while climbing.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-10)

Date fruit are oval-cylindrical, 3–7cm (1–3in) long, and 2–3cm (3⁄4–1+1⁄4in) diameter, and when ripe, range from bright red to bright yellow in colour, depending on variety. Dates contain a single [stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drupe "Drupe") (seed) about 2–2.5cm (3⁄4–1in) long and 6–8mm (1⁄4–5⁄16in) thick. Three main [cultivar groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultivar_group "Cultivar group") exist: _soft_ (e.g., [Medjool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medjool "Medjool")); _semi-dry_ (e.g., [Deglet Nour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deglet_Nour "Deglet Nour")), and _dry_ (e.g., [Thoory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoory "Thoory")).[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-11)

### Genome

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Genome")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Germination_of_Date_Palm.jpg/250px-Germination_of_Date_Palm.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Germination_of_Date_Palm.jpg) Germination of date palm

A draft genome of _P.dactylifera_ ( [Khalas variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalas_(date) "Khalas (date)")) was published in 2011[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-12) followed by more complete genome assemblies in 2013[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-13) and 2019.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Hazzouri-2019-14) The later study used long-read sequencing technology. With the release of this improved genome assembly, the researchers were able to map genes for fruit color and sugar content.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Hazzouri-2019-14) Researchers had also re-sequenced the genomes of several date varieties to develop the first single nucleotide polymorphism map of the date palm genome in 2015.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-15)

## Etymology

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Etymology")\]

The species name _dactylifera_ 'date-bearing' is [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin"), and is formed with the loanword _dactylus_ in Latin from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek "Ancient Greek") [δάκτυλος](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CE%B4%CE%AC%CE%BA%CF%84%CF%85%CE%BB%CE%BF%CF%82#Ancient_Greek "wikt:δάκτυλος")_daktylos_, which means 'date' (also 'finger'),[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-LSJ|da/ktulos|%CE%B4%CE%AC%CE%BA%CF%84%CF%85%CE%BB%CE%BF%CF%82|ref-16) and with the native Latin _fero_, which means 'to bear'.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-17) The fruit's English name (through [Old French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_French "Old French"), through Latin) comes from the aforementioned Greek word for 'finger'[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-LSJ|da/ktulos|%CE%B4%CE%AC%CE%BA%CF%84%CF%85%CE%BB%CE%BF%CF%82|ref-16) while itself cognate with the English word _[toe](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/toe#English "wikt:toe")_. An earlier word attested in Old English manuscripts was the [calque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calque "Calque") _fingerapple_ ( [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English_language "Old English language"): _fingeræppel_).[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-18)

The variety _[Phoenix dactylifera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_dactylifera "Phoenix dactylifera")_ var. _jubae_, now considered a synonym for _[P.canariensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_canariensis "Phoenix canariensis")_, was named after [King Juba II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juba_II "Juba II").[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-19)

## Distribution

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Distribution")\]

The place and timeline of origin of the date palm is uncertain because of long cultivation. According to some sources it probably originated from the [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent "Fertile Crescent") region straddling [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt") and [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia")[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Krueger-4) while others state that they are native to the [Persian Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf "Persian Gulf") area or derived from wild date palm from western India.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-20) Fossil records show that the date palm has existed for at least 50million years.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-NPRmedjool-21)

## Ecology

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Ecology")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/Dubas_sootymould2.jpg/250px-Dubas_sootymould2.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dubas_sootymould2.jpg) Sooty mould, nymph and larval cuticle of _[Ommatissus lybicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ommatissus_lybicus "Ommatissus lybicus")_, Oman

Further information: [List of date palm diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_date_palm_diseases "List of date palm diseases")

A major palm pest, the red palm beetle ( _[Rhynchophorus ferrugineus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhynchophorus_ferrugineus "Rhynchophorus ferrugineus")_), currently poses a significant threat to date production in parts of the Middle East as well as to iconic landscape specimens throughout the Mediterranean world. Another significant insect pest is _[Ommatissus lybicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ommatissus_lybicus "Ommatissus lybicus")_, sometimes called the "dubas bug", whose sap sucking results in [sooty mould](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sooty_mould "Sooty mould") formation.

In the 1920s, eleven healthy [Medjool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medjool "Medjool") palms were transferred from Morocco to the United States where they were tended by members of the [Chemehuevi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemehuevi "Chemehuevi") tribe\[ _[which?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Avoid_weasel_words "Wikipedia:Avoid weasel words")_\] in a remote region of Nevada. Nine of these survived and in 1935, cultivars were transferred to the U.S. Date Garden in [Indio, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indio,_California "Indio, California"). Eventually this stock was reintroduced to Africa and led to the U.S. production of dates in [Yuma, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuma,_Arizona "Yuma, Arizona"), and [Bard, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bard,_California "Bard, California").[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-ICN_Allen-22)

## Cultivation

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Cultivation")\]

Dates are a traditional crop throughout the Middle East and North Africa. Dates (especially [Medjool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medjool "Medjool") and [Deglet Nour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deglet_Nour "Deglet Nour")) are also cultivated in the southwestern United States, and in [Sonora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonora "Sonora") and [Baja California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baja_California "Baja California") in Mexico.

Date palms can take 4 to 8 years after planting before they will bear fruit, and start producing viable yields for commercial harvest between 7 and 10 years. Mature date palms can produce 70–140 kilograms (150–300 pounds)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-23)[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-24) of dates per harvest season. They do not all ripen at the same time so several harvests are required. To obtain fruit of marketable quality, the bunches of dates must be thinned and bagged or covered before ripening so that the remaining fruit grow larger and are protected from weather and animals, such as birds, that also like to eat them.

Date palms require well-drained deep [sandy loam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_loam "Sandy loam") soils with a pH of 8–11 (alkaline). The soil should have the ability to hold moisture and also be free of [calcium carbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_carbonate "Calcium carbonate").[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-25)

### Agricultural history

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Agricultural history")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Unripe_dates_on_a_palm_tree_in_Bahrain.jpg/250px-Unripe_dates_on_a_palm_tree_in_Bahrain.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Unripe_dates_on_a_palm_tree_in_Bahrain.jpg) Unripe dates on a palm tree in [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain "Bahrain")

Dates have been cultivated in the Middle East and the Indus Valley for thousands of years, and there is archaeological evidence of date cultivation in [Mehrgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehrgarh "Mehrgarh"), a Neolithic civilisation in western Pakistan, around 7000BCE[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-KenoyerHeuston2005-26) and in [eastern Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Arabia "Eastern Arabia") between 5530 and 5320 [calBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiocarbon_dating#Calibration "Radiocarbon dating").[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-27) Dates have been cultivated since ancient times from Mesopotamia to [prehistoric Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Egypt "Prehistoric Egypt"). The [ancient Egyptians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt") used the fruit to make date [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine "Wine") and ate dates at harvest.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-28) In the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant "Levant"), the earliest traces of dates appear during the [Chalcolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic "Chalcolithic") period, at sites such as [Nahal Mishmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahal_Mishmar "Nahal Mishmar") (Israel) and [Teleilat el-Ghassul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teleilat_el-Ghassul "Teleilat el-Ghassul") (Jordan).[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Shafer-Elliott-2022-29) Evidence of cultivation is continually found throughout later civilisations in the Indus Valley, including the [Harappan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harappa "Harappa") period from 2600 to 1900BCE.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-KenoyerHeuston2005-26)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/38/Mazafati_dates_-_whole%2C_halved_and_seed.jpg/250px-Mazafati_dates_-_whole%2C_halved_and_seed.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mazafati_dates_-_whole,_halved_and_seed.jpg) Mazafati dates

One [cultivar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultivar "Cultivar"), the [Judean date palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judean_date_palm "Judean date palm"), is renowned for its long-lived [orthodox seed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_seed "Orthodox seed"), which successfully sprouted after accidental storage for 2,000 years.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-30) In total seven seeds about 2000 years old have sprouted and turned into trees named Methuselah, Hannah, Adam, Judith, Boaz, Jonah and Uriel.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-31) The upper survival time limit of properly stored seeds remains unknown.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-32) A genomic study from New York University Abu Dhabi Center for Genomics and Systems Biology showed that domesticated date palm varieties from North Africa, including well-known varieties such as [Medjool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medjool "Medjool") and [Deglet Nour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deglet_Nour "Deglet Nour"), share large parts of their genome with Middle East date palms and the Cretan wild palms, _[P.theophrasti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_theophrasti "Phoenix theophrasti")_, as well as Indian wild palms, _[Phoenix sylvestris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_sylvestris "Phoenix sylvestris")_.

An article on date palm tree cultivation is contained in [Ibn al-'Awwam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_al-'Awwam "Ibn al-'Awwam")'s 12th-century agricultural work, _Book on Agriculture_.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-33) In later times, traders spread dates around southwest Asia, northern Africa, and Spain. Dates were introduced into California by the Spaniards by 1769, existing by then around [Mission San Diego de Alcalá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_San_Diego_de_Alcal%C3%A1 "Mission San Diego de Alcalá"), and were introduced to Mexico as early as the 16th century.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-34)

### Cultivars

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Cultivars")\]

Main article: [List of date cultivars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_date_cultivars "List of date cultivars")

A large number of [date cultivars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_cultivar "Date cultivar") and varieties emerged through history of its cultivation, but the exact number is difficult to assess. Hussain and El-Zeid[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-36) (1975) have reported 400 varieties, while Nixon[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-37) (1954) named around 250. Most of those are limited to a particular region, and only a few dozen have attained broader commercial importance. The most renowned cultivars worldwide include [Deglet Noor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deglet_Noor "Deglet Noor"), originally of Algeria; Yahidi and Hallawi of Iraq; [Medjool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medjool "Medjool") of Morocco; [Mazafati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazafati "Mazafati") of Iran.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-HuiBarta2008-38)

## Production

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Production")\]

In 2024, world production of dates was 9.9 million [tonnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonne "Tonne"), led by Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Algeria, and Iran, together accounting for 63% of the total (table).

Deglet noor dates

Deglet noor dates are 21% water, 75% [carbohydrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbohydrate "Carbohydrate") (63% sugars and 8% [dietary fibre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietary_fibre "Dietary fibre")), 2% [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_(nutrient) "Protein (nutrient)"), and less than 1% [fat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat "Fat") (table). In a reference amount of 100 grams (3.5oz), dates supply 1,180 kilojoules (280 kilocalories) of [food energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_energy "Food energy"), and are a rich source (20% or more of the [Daily Value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Value "Daily Value"), DV) of [potassium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_in_biology "Potassium in biology") (22% DV) and a moderate source of [pantothenic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantothenic_acid "Pantothenic acid"), [vitamin B6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_B6 "Vitamin B6"), and the [dietary minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_(nutrient) "Mineral (nutrient)") [magnesium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesium_in_biology "Magnesium in biology") and [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese_in_biology "Manganese in biology") (10–19% DV), with other [micronutrients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronutrient "Micronutrient") in low amounts (table). The primary carbohydrates are [monosaccharides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monosaccharide "Monosaccharide")– [glucose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose "Glucose") (20%), [fructose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fructose "Fructose") (20%), and [sucrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sucrose "Sucrose") (24%) (table).

The [glycemic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycemic_index "Glycemic index") (GI) for different varieties of date palm fruit is in the range of 38–71, with 53 on average,[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-alkaabi-41) indicating dates are a relatively low GI food source.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-42)

### Fruit

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Fruit")\]

Dry or soft dates are eaten in-the-hand, or may be stoned and stuffed with fillings such as [almonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond "Almond"), [walnuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walnut "Walnut"), [pecans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecan "Pecan"), candied [orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_(fruit) "Orange (fruit)") and [lemon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemon "Lemon") [peel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peel_(fruit) "Peel (fruit)"), [tahini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahini "Tahini"), [marzipan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marzipan "Marzipan") or [cream cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cream_cheese "Cream cheese"). Stoned dates are also referred to as _pitted dates_. Partially dried stoned dates may be glazed with [glucose syrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose_syrup "Glucose syrup") for use as a snack food. Dates can also be chopped and used in a range of sweet and savory dishes, from [tajines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajine "Tajine") (tagines) in [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco "Morocco") to [puddings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pudding "Pudding"), _[ka'ak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ka'ak "Ka'ak")_ (types of Arab cookies) and other dessert items. Date nut bread, a type of cake, is very popular in the United States, especially around holidays. Dates are also processed into cubes, paste called _'ajwa_, spread, [date syrup or "honey"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_honey "Date honey") called "dibs" or _[rub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub_(syrup) "Rub (syrup)")_ in Libya, powder ( [date sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_sugar "Date sugar")), [vinegar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinegar "Vinegar") or [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_beverage "Alcoholic beverage"). Vinegar made from dates was a traditional product of the [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East").[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-43)[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-44) Recent innovations include [chocolate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chocolate "Chocolate")-covered dates and products such as sparkling date juice, used in some Islamic countries as a non-alcoholic version of [champagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champagne_(wine) "Champagne (wine)"), for special occasions and religious times such as [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan "Ramadan"). When Muslims break fast in the [evening meal of Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iftar "Iftar"), it is traditional to eat a date first.

Imported chopped dates are added to, or form the main basis of, a variety of traditional English dessert recipes including [sticky toffee pudding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sticky_toffee_pudding "Sticky toffee pudding") and [Christmas pudding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_pudding "Christmas pudding"). They are particularly available to eat whole at [Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas "Christmas") time. Dates are one of the ingredients of [HP Sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP_Sauce "HP Sauce"), a popular English condiment.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-45)

In Southeast Spain (where a large date plantation exists including [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO")-protected [Palmeral of Elche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmeral_of_Elche "Palmeral of Elche")) dates (usually stoned with fried almond) are served wrapped in [bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacon "Bacon") and shallow-fried. In Israel date syrup, termed _silan_, is used while cooking chicken and also for sweets and desserts, and as a honey substitute. Dates are one of the ingredients of _[jallab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jallab "Jallab")_, a Middle Eastern fruit syrup. In Pakistan, a viscous, thick syrup made from the ripe fruit is used as a coating for leather bags and pipes to prevent leaking.

#### Forks

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Forks")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Date_forks_in_lizard_stand.jpg/250px-Date_forks_in_lizard_stand.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Date_forks_in_lizard_stand.jpg) Antique date forks in rack

In the past, sticky dates were served using specialised small forks having two metal tines, called _[daddelgaffel](https://no.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaffel#Gaffeltyper "nb:Gaffel")_ in Scandinavia.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-46) Some designs were patented.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-47) These have generally been replaced by an inexpensive pale-colored knobbled plastic fork that resembles a date branch, which is traditionally included with numerous brands of prepackaged trays of dates, though this practice has declined in response to increased use of resealable packaging and calls for fewer [single-use plastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disposable_product "Disposable product").

### Seeds

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Seeds")\]

Date palm seeds contain 0.56–5.4% lauric acid and can also be processed chemically as a source of [oxalic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxalic_acid "Oxalic acid"). Their oil, which contains [lauric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauric_acid "Lauric acid") (36%) and [oleic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleic_acid "Oleic acid") (41%), is suitable to be used in cosmetics and dermatological applications. The seeds can be soaked and ground up for [animal feed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_feed "Animal feed"). They can also be ground and used in the manner of [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee "Coffee") beans, or as an additive to coffee. Experimental studies have shown that feeding mice with the [aqueous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqueous "Aqueous") [extract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extract "Extract") of date seeds exhibit anti- [genotoxic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genotoxic "Genotoxic") effects and reduce DNA damage induced by [N-nitroso-N-methylurea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-nitroso-N-methylurea "N-nitroso-N-methylurea").[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Diab_KA-48)

### Fruit clusters

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Fruit clusters")\]

Stripped fruit clusters are used as brooms. Recently, the floral stalks have been found to be of ornamental value in households.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-49)

### Sap

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Sap")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/Sweet_sap_from_date_palm.JPG/250px-Sweet_sap_from_date_palm.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sweet_sap_from_date_palm.JPG) Sweet [sap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_sap "Plant sap") tapped from date palm in [West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal "West Bengal"), India

Apart from _P.dactylifera_, wild date palms such as _[Phoenix sylvestris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_sylvestris "Phoenix sylvestris")_ and _[Phoenix reclinata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_reclinata "Phoenix reclinata")_, depending on the region, can be also tapped for sap.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-50)

The consumption of raw date palm sap is one of the means by which the deadly [Nipah virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nipah_virus "Nipah virus") spreads from bats to humans.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Goats_&_Soda-51) The virus can be [inactivated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inactivated_vaccine "Inactivated vaccine") by boiling the sap down to [molasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molasses "Molasses").[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Goats_&_Soda-51)

### Leaves

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Leaves")\]

In North Africa, date palm leaves are commonly used for making huts. Mature leaves are also made into mats, screens, baskets, and fans. Processed leaves can be used for [insulating board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_insulation "Thermal insulation"). Dried leaf [petioles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petiole_(botany) "Petiole (botany)") are a source of [cellulose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellulose "Cellulose") pulp, used for walking sticks, brooms, fishing floats, and fuel. Leaf sheaths are prized for their scent, and fibre from them is also used for rope, coarse cloth, and large hats.

Young date leaves are cooked and eaten as a vegetable, as is the terminal bud or heart, though its removal kills the palm. The finely ground seeds are mixed with [flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flour "Flour") to make bread in times of scarcity. The flowers of the date palm are also edible. Traditionally the female flowers are the most available for sale and weigh 300–400 grams (10+1⁄2–14oz). The flower buds are used in salad or ground with dried fish to make a [condiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condiment "Condiment") for bread.

## In culture

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: In culture")\]

Further information: [Palm branch (symbol)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_branch_(symbol) "Palm branch (symbol)") and [Plants in Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plants_in_Islam "Plants in Islam")

Dates are mentioned more than 50 times in the [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible "Bible") and 20 times in the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran "Quran"). Date palms holds great significance in [Abrahamic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religions "Abrahamic religions"). The tree was heavily cultivated as a food source in [ancient Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_ancient_Israel_and_Judah "History of ancient Israel and Judah") where [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism") and subsequently [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") developed.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-James_Hastings_1909-52) Date palm leaves are used for [Palm Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Sunday "Palm Sunday") in the Christian religion.

### Ancient Rome

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Ancient Rome")\]

In [Ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome "Ancient Rome"), the palm fronds used in [triumphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_triumph "Roman triumph") processions to symbolise victory were most likely those of _P.dactylifera_.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-53) The date palm was a popular garden plant in Roman [peristyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peristyle "Peristyle") gardens, though it would not bear fruit in the more temperate climate of Italy.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Linda_Farrar_1998_141-54) It is recognisable in frescoes from [Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii "Pompeii") and elsewhere in Italy, including a garden scene from the House of the Wedding of Alexander.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Linda_Farrar_1998_141-54)

### Ancient Israel and Judaism

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Ancient Israel and Judaism")\]

The date palm is one of the " [Seven Species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Species "Seven Species")" ( _shivat haminim_) traditionally associated with the [Land of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Israel "Land of Israel") and revered in [Jewish tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_culture "Jewish culture").[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Shafer-Elliott-2022-29)[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-55) The date palm has historically been considered a symbol of [Judea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judea_(Roman_province) "Judea (Roman province)") and the [Jewish people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews").[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-56)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Ancient_Coin.jpeg/250px-Ancient_Coin.jpeg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ancient_Coin.jpeg) 2nd-century Jewish coin minted in [Judea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judea "Judea"), featuring a palm tree motif

The date palm is featured prominently in the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible "Hebrew Bible"). Scholars interpret the "honey" mentioned in [Exodus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Exodus "Book of Exodus") 3, describing the [Promised Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promised_Land "Promised Land") as "flowing with milk and honey", as a reference not to bee honey but to date syrup.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Shafer-Elliott-2022-29) Palm trees or their parts are mentioned in the [Torah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah "Torah") in connection with locations such as [Elim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elim_(place) "Elim (place)"), [Jericho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jericho "Jericho") ("the city of palm trees"), and at [Deborah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah "Deborah")'s place of judgment in [Judges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Judges "Book of Judges").[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Shafer-Elliott-2022-29) They are also listed among the agricultural blessings of the land ( [Joel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Joel "Book of Joel") 1:12),[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Shafer-Elliott-2022-29) and their imagery is employed in [biblical poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_poetry "Biblical poetry") to symbolise moral uprightness and stature, including in [Psalm 92](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalm_92 "Psalm 92"):12: "The righteous shall flourish like the palm tree", and in [Song of Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_of_Songs "Song of Songs") 7:8, where physical beauty is compared to the tree's form.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Shafer-Elliott-2022-29) According to the Bible, carved palm motifs decorated [Solomon's Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon's_Temple "Solomon's Temple") (1 Kings 6:29, 32, 35) and were part of the prophetic vision of the [restored temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezekiel's_Temple "Ezekiel's Temple") in [Ezekiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Ezekiel "Book of Ezekiel") (Ezek. 41:18–20).[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Shafer-Elliott-2022-29) Outside of biblical texts, palm designs appear in the stone capitals and pilasters at several ancient [Israelite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israelites "Israelites") sites, including [Hazor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Hazor "Tel Hazor"), [Megiddo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Megiddo "Tel Megiddo"), [Samaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaria_(ancient_city) "Samaria (ancient city)"), [Dan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_(ancient_city) "Dan (ancient city)"), and [Ramat Rahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramat_Rachel "Ramat Rachel").[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Shafer-Elliott-2022-29)

The palm tree became a broader emblem of Judea and its people, appearing on Jewish coinage and temple decorations. Palm branches occurred as iconography in sculpture ornamenting the [Second Jewish Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Jewish_Temple "Second Jewish Temple") in Jerusalem, on Jewish coins, and in the sculpture of [synagogues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synagogue "Synagogue"). In the Jewish festival of _[Sukkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukkot "Sukkot")_ (Feast of Tabernacles), date palm leaves serve two important functions. The _[lulav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lulav "Lulav")_, a closed frond of the date palm, is one of the [Four Species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_species "Four species") waved during prayers as part of the ritual observance.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-57)[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-James_Hastings_1909-52) Palm leaves are also commonly used as _[s'chach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S'chach "S'chach")_, the natural roofing material placed atop the temporary outdoor hut known as a _[sukkah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukkah "Sukkah")_.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-58)

### Islam

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Islam")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/Meharban_dates_at_a_store.jpg/250px-Meharban_dates_at_a_store.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Meharban_dates_at_a_store.jpg) Meharban dates for sale at a store in preparation for Ramadan

In the Quran, [Allah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allah "Allah") instructs _Maryām_ (the Virgin Mary) to eat dates during labour pains when she gives birth to Isa (Jesus).[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-59) In [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") culture, dates and yogurt or milk are traditionally the first foods consumed for [Iftar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iftar "Iftar") after the sun has set during [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan "Ramadan").

### Mandaeism

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Date_palm&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Mandaeism")\]

In [Mandaeism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeism "Mandaeism"), the date palm ( [Mandaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaic_language "Mandaic language"): _sindirka_, which can refer to both the tree and its fruit[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-Secret_Adam-60)) symbolises the [cosmic tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_tree "Cosmic tree") and is often associated with the cosmic wellspring (Mandaic: _aina_). The date palm, associated with masculinity, and wellspring, associated with femininity, are often mentioned together as heavenly symbols in [Mandaean texts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaean_texts "Mandaean texts").[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm#cite_note-61)
