## Identity

Silver birch is a medium-sized, short-lived broadleaved tree that occurs throughout most of Europe and is most abundant in its northern regions. [JRC-BIRCH](sources/JRC-BIRCH.md)
In southern Europe it is confined to mountainous areas, because it does not tolerate prolonged summer drought. [JRC-BIRCH](sources/JRC-BIRCH.md)
It is light-demanding and intolerant of shade, and its abundant seed production, its low demands on soil quality and its wind-distributed winged fruits together make it a pioneer early in secondary succession. [JRC-BIRCH](sources/JRC-BIRCH.md)
It nonetheless grows best on fairly fertile, light, well-drained soils, particularly acidic ones. [JRC-BIRCH](sources/JRC-BIRCH.md)
Beyond Europe it is present across central-northern Asia, from the Caucasus through Siberia into China and Japan, and it is well adapted to cold climates. [OSU-BIRCH](sources/OSU-BIRCH.md)
In North America it is not native but a widely cultivated tree that has persisted or become locally naturalized in several areas. [FNA-BIRCH](sources/FNA-BIRCH.md)

## Size and growth

The European atlas describes it as a medium-sized tree and gives a maximum above the top of the interval the table below gates on. [JRC-BIRCH](sources/JRC-BIRCH.md)
The Oregon State landscape description puts the usual height of a planted tree in the lower part of that interval, with occasional individuals much taller. [OSU-BIRCH](sources/OSU-BIRCH.md)
Birch trees are short-lived, and an individual only rarely lives much beyond the species' usual span. [JRC-BIRCH](sources/JRC-BIRCH.md)
They can grow rapidly, on poor soils as well as good ones, and that vigour is part of what makes them thrive as pioneers. [JRC-BIRCH](sources/JRC-BIRCH.md)
In denser stands a birch does not flower until it is considerably older, while a free-standing tree can flower much earlier. [JRC-BIRCH](sources/JRC-BIRCH.md)
Seed production varies with tree age and site conditions, and the crop of viable seed peaks only every few years. [JRC-BIRCH](sources/JRC-BIRCH.md)

## Crown and habit

The crown is light, a canopy of small serrated leaves. [JRC-BIRCH](sources/JRC-BIRCH.md)
The branches characteristically droop, and that is one of the features separating this birch from the downy birch, whose branches grow upwards or horizontally. [JRC-BIRCH](sources/JRC-BIRCH.md)
The Oregon State description gives the same habit as a graceful pendulous branching, with the young twigs thin and usually pendulous, and the outline pyramidal becoming rounded. [OSU-BIRCH](sources/OSU-BIRCH.md)
Flora of North America records spreading crowns, pendulous branches, and hairless twigs usually dotted with small resinous glands. [FNA-BIRCH](sources/FNA-BIRCH.md)
The same account notes that the trunks are usually several, so a single clean bole is not the only form the species takes. [FNA-BIRCH](sources/FNA-BIRCH.md)

## Bark

The bark of mature trunks and branches is creamy to silvery white and smooth, exfoliating as long strands. [FNA-BIRCH](sources/FNA-BIRCH.md)
On a young tree it is brown, turning silvery-white at maturity and then darkening and developing fissures with age. [JRC-BIRCH](sources/JRC-BIRCH.md)
The white surface carries dark lenticels, horizontally expanded, and dark scars where shoots have dropped. [FNA-BIRCH](sources/FNA-BIRCH.md) [JRC-BIRCH](sources/JRC-BIRCH.md)
The bark of this birch is a brighter white and shinier than that of the downy birch. [JRC-BIRCH](sources/JRC-BIRCH.md)
The Oregon State description adds that the whitish trunk bark does not peel as easily as the paper birch's, and that the trunk becomes mostly black with age. [OSU-BIRCH](sources/OSU-BIRCH.md)
Flora of North America treats the peeling bark as the easiest mark separating it from the closely allied Betula populifolia. [FNA-BIRCH](sources/FNA-BIRCH.md)

## Leaves

The leaves are simple and alternate. [OSU-BIRCH](sources/OSU-BIRCH.md)
The blade is broadly ovate to rhombic, sometimes diamond-shaped, wedge-shaped or truncate at the base, and slenderly tapered to an acuminate apex. [FNA-BIRCH](sources/FNA-BIRCH.md) [OSU-BIRCH](sources/OSU-BIRCH.md)
The margin is coarsely, sharply and unequally doubly serrate, and the blade ends in a fine point. [FNA-BIRCH](sources/FNA-BIRCH.md) [JRC-BIRCH](sources/JRC-BIRCH.md)
Both surfaces are dotted with minute resinous glands, and the underside is glabrous to sparsely pubescent. [OSU-BIRCH](sources/OSU-BIRCH.md) [FNA-BIRCH](sources/FNA-BIRCH.md)
Autumn colour is yellow. [OSU-BIRCH](sources/OSU-BIRCH.md)
The long slender petiole the record's anatomy describes rests on the Trees and Shrubs Online account, whose text this folder could not retrieve. [TSO-BIRCH](sources/TSO-BIRCH.md)
