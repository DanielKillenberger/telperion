## Identity

European beech is a large deciduous tree and one of the most important and widespread broadleaved trees in Europe. [JRC-BEECH](sources/JRC-BEECH.md)
Its natural range extends from southern Scandinavia to Sicily and from Spain in the west to north-west Turkey in the east. [JRC-BEECH](sources/JRC-BEECH.md)
It is centred on central and western Europe, where it forms extensive forests at lower elevations, blends into mixed coniferous forest higher up and eventually gives way to conifers entirely. [TSO-BEECH](sources/TSO-BEECH.md)
It is native in woods on well-drained soils, especially in hills and mountains, and is not naturally at home on poorly drained soils or low-lying plains, though it is extensively planted everywhere. [TSO-BEECH](sources/TSO-BEECH.md)
It is the most shade-tolerant broadleaved tree in its range and casts a deep shade of its own. [JRC-BEECH](sources/JRC-BEECH.md) [PACKHAM-BEECH](sources/PACKHAM-BEECH.md)
Native to Europe and long cultivated, it also comes in a number of named selections beside the wild type described here. [OSU-BEECH](sources/OSU-BEECH.md)

## Size and growth

Beech is a large tree of variable form, usually single-stemmed, whose typical mature height and its occasional maximum in near-natural conditions are the range the table below records. [PACKHAM-BEECH](sources/PACKHAM-BEECH.md)
In contrast to many other tree species it is able to maintain a high rate of growth until a relatively mature age. [JRC-BEECH](sources/JRC-BEECH.md)
Trees drawn up in woodland or near-woodland attain the greatest heights, and commentators have long distinguished them from trees grown in the open, in parkland or on a lawn. [TSO-BEECH](sources/TSO-BEECH.md)
Parkland and coppiced trees are considerably thicker in the bole than the diameter the table below gives. [TSO-BEECH](sources/TSO-BEECH.md)
It is not an especially long-lived tree in Britain and Ireland, where old trees are liable to die suddenly without much warning. [TSO-BEECH](sources/TSO-BEECH.md)

## Crown and habit

Grown in the open as a landscape tree, the crown is dense and pyramidal to oval. [OSU-BEECH](sources/OSU-BEECH.md)
The tree almost invariably has a single stem, and the bole is characteristically close to cylindrical. [PACKHAM-BEECH](sources/PACKHAM-BEECH.md)
Trunks of trees grown in open situations bear massive branches quite low down, and these may become so long and heavy that some touch the ground, sometimes layering and sending up vertical stems. [PACKHAM-BEECH](sources/PACKHAM-BEECH.md)
Architecture varies greatly between open and closed canopies: in shade the branches adopt a monolayer of large, horizontal, regularly dispersed leaves, while in sun they adopt a multilayer arrangement with the leaves held more erectly. [PACKHAM-BEECH](sources/PACKHAM-BEECH.md)
Compared to oak, beech branches are slow to die but are shed very rapidly once dead, which matches its greater shade tolerance. [PACKHAM-BEECH](sources/PACKHAM-BEECH.md)
Form is readily modified by environment, as late spring frosts that injure terminal buds increase forking, and opening a dense canopy leads to rapid and extensive upward reorientation of the branches. [PACKHAM-BEECH](sources/PACKHAM-BEECH.md)

## Bark

The bark is smooth and silver-grey to pale grey, often tinged green by epiphytic algae and only occasionally slightly roughened. [PACKHAM-BEECH](sources/PACKHAM-BEECH.md) [TSO-BEECH](sources/TSO-BEECH.md)
It is relatively thin over a virtually cylindrical bole, and on old trunks it develops an elephant-hide appearance. [PACKHAM-BEECH](sources/PACKHAM-BEECH.md) [OSU-BEECH](sources/OSU-BEECH.md)
Little corky tissue is produced, so the bark stays smooth, or at most slightly roughened, and is shed in dust-like fragments. [PACKHAM-BEECH](sources/PACKHAM-BEECH.md)
The cork cambium normally remains active throughout the life of the tree and grows laterally to accommodate the increasing girth of the trunk, which is why graffiti scars it for the long term. [PACKHAM-BEECH](sources/PACKHAM-BEECH.md)
The thin bark provides little protection from fire and can also be damaged by stripping. [JRC-BEECH](sources/JRC-BEECH.md)

## Leaves

The leaves are simple and alternate, oval to elliptic, with an acute apex and a rounded base, and each blade is carried on a short downy petiole. [PACKHAM-BEECH](sources/PACKHAM-BEECH.md) [TSO-BEECH](sources/TSO-BEECH.md) [OSU-BEECH](sources/OSU-BEECH.md)
The blade is usually entire, with a wavy margin and remote, very fine toothing or scalloping that is more pronounced near the tip. [PACKHAM-BEECH](sources/PACKHAM-BEECH.md) [TSO-BEECH](sources/TSO-BEECH.md)
Its veins run in prominent pairs on either side of the midrib and are raised beneath, with short teeth at the vein ends on the margin. [PACKHAM-BEECH](sources/PACKHAM-BEECH.md) [JRC-BEECH](sources/JRC-BEECH.md)
The upper surface is lustrous dark green and the underside is paler. [OSU-BEECH](sources/OSU-BEECH.md) [JRC-BEECH](sources/JRC-BEECH.md)
Young leaves are silky and fringed with hairs, and lose that hairiness as they mature. [OSU-BEECH](sources/OSU-BEECH.md) [PACKHAM-BEECH](sources/PACKHAM-BEECH.md)
Trimmed individuals frequently bear very much larger leaves than untrimmed ones do. [PACKHAM-BEECH](sources/PACKHAM-BEECH.md)
