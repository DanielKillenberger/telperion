## Identity

European ash is a medium-sized deciduous tree of the family Oleaceae, and Kew accepts _Fraxinus excelsior_ L. as its name. [J1](sources/J1.md) [KEW-ASH](sources/KEW-ASH.md)
Its native range covers the European temperate zone, running from the Atlantic coast east to the Volga and reaching the Caucasus and Iran. [J1](sources/J1.md) [BALTIC-ASH](sources/BALTIC-ASH.md) [KEW-ASH](sources/KEW-ASH.md)
It is absent from the driest Mediterranean areas, because it does not tolerate extended summer drought, and from the northern boreal regions. [J1](sources/J1.md)
It rarely forms pure stands, occurring more often as scattered trees or in clumps within mixed broadleaved forest, though it can sometimes come close to a pure stand. [J1](sources/J1.md) [THOMAS-ASH](sources/THOMAS-ASH.md)
Ash dieback, caused by the fungus _Hymenoscyphus fraxineus_, has spread through eastern, northern and central Europe and has killed the great majority of ash trees in many countries. [J1](sources/J1.md) [BALTIC-ASH](sources/BALTIC-ASH.md)
The Kew taxon page copied here carries the accepted name, the native range, the synonyms and the publications the database follows, but no description of the tree's form, so where the measurement table cites it this copy holds no passage behind the citation. [KEW-ASH](sources/KEW-ASH.md)
The same Oregon State University page is copied into this folder twice under two source ids, so a claim cited to both still rests on one description. [O1](sources/O1.md) [OSU-ASH](sources/OSU-ASH.md)

## Size and growth

Common ash usually grows to about the height the table below records, and only occasionally reaches more. [J1](sources/J1.md)
It grows fastest and attains its largest dimensions on fertile, moist but well-drained sites, while exposure to strong winds and to dry or compacted soil reduces both the rate of growth and the final size of the tree. [THOMAS-ASH](sources/THOMAS-ASH.md)
On rich alluvial soils it reaches a far greater height and trunk diameter than in the lowland forests where a more modest height is common. [THOMAS-ASH](sources/THOMAS-ASH.md)
Young trees grow very rapidly, although full overhead light is needed for them to develop vigorously. [J1](sources/J1.md)
In Polish forest stands the mean diameter at breast height, the height and the volume of dominant and codominant ash all rise strongly with age, and the forest site type shifts the sizes reached. [BALTIC-ASH](sources/BALTIC-ASH.md)
The German yield tables the record draws on give the average growth course of moderately thinned pure stands by age and yield class, and for mixed stands they are stated to give only rough orientation values. [E1](sources/E1.md)

## Crown and habit

The crown is domed and open, carried on ascending branches. [J1](sources/J1.md)
The Oregon State University description makes the tree round-headed and broad spreading, of a width equal to or even somewhat greater than its height. [OSU-ASH](sources/OSU-ASH.md) [O1](sources/O1.md)
Thomas instead describes a narrow crown on British trees, one often reduced to a shrub in exposed positions. [THOMAS-ASH](sources/THOMAS-ASH.md)
Branching of the shoot is monopodial and runs to a low number of branch orders, and while the tree is young it invests in vertical height growth and self-prunes its side branches rapidly. [THOMAS-ASH](sources/THOMAS-ASH.md)
Only as it approaches maturity does the canopy widen into a rounded rhombic crown, which in the south of England is consistently wider than those of its main competitor trees at every trunk diameter. [THOMAS-ASH](sources/THOMAS-ASH.md)
Forking of the stem is undesirable in timber, and can be caused by frost injury, water stress or animal browsing. [J1](sources/J1.md)

## Bark

The bark is pale grey and smooth on young trees, and thickens and develops fissures with age. [J1](sources/J1.md) [THOMAS-ASH](sources/THOMAS-ASH.md)
Thomas calls the bark of ash thin, and cites a measured thickness on trees of moderate trunk diameter similar to that of _Fagus sylvatica_ and _Acer pseudoplatanus_. [THOMAS-ASH](sources/THOMAS-ASH.md)
Ash dieback shows on the bark as a reddish discolouration in the lower portion of the stem. [J1](sources/J1.md)
On scree and unstable slopes the trunk may become prostrate at the base and put out adventitious roots on its underside, so the lowest part of the trunk can look like root rather than stem. [THOMAS-ASH](sources/THOMAS-ASH.md)
The bark was traditionally used to tan calf skin. [J1](sources/J1.md)

## Leaves

The leaves are opposite and odd-pinnate, their leaflets sessile and serrated, with a leaflet count and a whole-leaf length the measurement table records. [J1](sources/J1.md) [THOMAS-ASH](sources/THOMAS-ASH.md)
One leaf of an opposite pair may be displaced vertically or, more rarely, be missing altogether. [THOMAS-ASH](sources/THOMAS-ASH.md)
The leaflets are lanceolate to ovate and taper to a fine point, dark green above and lighter below, with soft shaggy hairs on the midrib beneath. [THOMAS-ASH](sources/THOMAS-ASH.md) [OSU-ASH](sources/OSU-ASH.md)
They develop relatively late in spring, after the flowers have opened. [J1](sources/J1.md)
Because the canopy is of low density the adult foliage carries sun leaves only, while shaded saplings bear both sun and shade leaves, the latter thinner and with a single distinct palisade layer. [THOMAS-ASH](sources/THOMAS-ASH.md)
The terminal winter buds are black. [THOMAS-ASH](sources/THOMAS-ASH.md) [OSU-ASH](sources/OSU-ASH.md)
Autumn colour runs from green to yellow. [OSU-ASH](sources/OSU-ASH.md) [O1](sources/O1.md)
