## Identity

Norway spruce is a woody, needled evergreen tree of the pine family, Pinaceae. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md)
It is native to northern and central Europe, and it has been introduced well beyond that range, including across the northern United States. [OSU-SPRUCE](sources/OSU-SPRUCE.md) [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md)
It is the typical Christmas tree in Britain and the most widely used spruce in North America for horticultural purposes. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md) [OSU-SPRUCE](sources/OSU-SPRUCE.md)
It wants full sun and a moderately moist, sandy, acid, well-drained soil, and it dislikes heat, wet feet and hot dry summers. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md) [OSU-SPRUCE](sources/OSU-SPRUCE.md)
Mutations of the species have given rise to numerous cultivars, many of them dwarf forms, and this record describes the species and none of them. [OSU-SPRUCE](sources/OSU-SPRUCE.md) [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md)

## Size and growth

Growth is rapid. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md)
A mature tree reaches the height and the spread the table below records. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md) [OSU-SPRUCE](sources/OSU-SPRUCE.md)
Its root system is shallow. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md)
It prefers a cool summer climate and is best suited to a northern one, and it does poorly in the southern United States. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md)
Where the climate suits it, it is planted in large home landscapes, woodland, meadow and recreational ground, and it serves as a windbreak or a screen in colder places. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md)

## Crown and habit

The habit is conical and pyramidal. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md) [OSU-SPRUCE](sources/OSU-SPRUCE.md)
The primary branches are mostly upturned, and slender secondary branches hang below them. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md) [OSU-SPRUCE](sources/OSU-SPRUCE.md)
Both pages tie that drooping to age: the branches are upright and the tree stiff when young, pendulous and more graceful as it matures. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md) [OSU-SPRUCE](sources/OSU-SPRUCE.md)
The stem note on the same toolbox page says instead that the secondary branches rarely appear pendulous with maturity, so its own descriptions do not agree on how far the secondaries hang. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md)
At maturity the crown may thin and become open, which some consider unsightly. [OSU-SPRUCE](sources/OSU-SPRUCE.md)

## Bark

The bark is purplish, green or silvery brown, and the same record lists dark brown, light brown and light grey among its colours. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md)
Young bark is thin, and as the tree matures it thickens and carries flaky scales. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md)
Sticky resin is found on the bark, and the genus name is thought to come from the Latin word for pitch. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md)
The landscape-plants page carries no bark description at all, so everything the record holds about bark rests on the single passage above it. [OSU-SPRUCE](sources/OSU-SPRUCE.md)

## Leaves

The leaves are individual needles, evergreen, glossy and green. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md)
Each needle is four-sided and sabre-shaped, stiff and sharp pointed, incurved towards its tip, and it carries stomatal lines along its sides. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md) [OSU-SPRUCE](sources/OSU-SPRUCE.md)
The needles are attached by tiny pegs at their base and set in rows along the branch like a hairbrush. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md)
They stand all around the stem, those on the upper side of a twig pointing forward and overlapping, those below spreading out from it. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md) [OSU-SPRUCE](sources/OSU-SPRUCE.md)
Both pages state a needle length, and the table above records the range the packet took from them. [NCSU-SPRUCE](sources/NCSU-SPRUCE.md) [OSU-SPRUCE](sources/OSU-SPRUCE.md)
